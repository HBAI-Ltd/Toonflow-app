# Storyboard-table narrative technique · Sci-Fi Post-Apocalypse · technique reference

---

## 1. What the storyboard table is for

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into shot language. The fields of the form are set by the director as the project requires (shot number, shot size, camera movement, duration, characters, events, dialogue, lighting, emotion, transitions and so on); what follows is only a technique reference for the sci-fi post-apocalypse narrative genre.

---

## 2. Choosing the shot size

- **远景 is the soul shot size of the post-apocalypse** — 远景 (long shot) is used far more often in sci-fi post-apocalypse than in any other genre. The whole of the ruins, wasteland without end, the macro shock of the relics of civilisation are the bedrock of post-apocalyptic narrative
- **The survival 特写** — a 特写 (close-up) in the post-apocalypse focuses not on the beautiful but on the real: split lips, dirt under the nails, blood seeping through a bandage, the last mouthful of water swilling at the bottom of the bottle. 特写 is the magnifying glass on the pressure of survival
- **Shot sizes for technological relics** — for abandoned technology (a rusting robot, a dead screen, a collapsed antenna tower) use a 中景 (medium shot) to state the whole plus a 特写 to focus the detail; two shots complete the narrative of "once advanced, now discarded"
- **Extreme restraint with 大特写** — in the post-apocalypse, 大特写 (extreme close-up) is saved for "the object with the most narrative force": a photograph, an unsent letter, a child's shoe. At most once in a whole passage
- **The establishing shot needs "shock + information"** — post-apocalyptic establishing must carry both the shock and the survival information at once: the scale of the ruins (shock) plus whether there is a threat or a resource (information). Done in 1-2 远景 shots

---

## 3. Camera-movement rhythm

- **Handheld as the base** — 50% or more of the shots on a slight handheld, carrying the instability of survival
- **A small shake when safe** — in rest-stop and camp passages the handheld shake is minimal, almost imperceptible, but carries a trace more "living breath" than a tripod
- **A clear shake when in danger** — exploring an unknown space or hearing an unidentified sound, the handheld shake increases, mirroring the character's alertness and unease
- **Slow move = exploring / searching** — an extremely slow lateral move simulates a character advancing with care; the slower it goes, the stronger the suspense
- **Fast push in = crisis / discovery** — when a threat appears or something important is found, push in hard and fast with the impact at maximum
- **Slow pull back = revealing the barrenness** — pulling slowly from a detail out to 远景, the endlessness of the ruins opening out step by step. It is the signature camera movement of post-apocalyptic narrative

---

## 4. Duration control

- **Gazing at the ruins** — give a 远景 shot of the ruins a full 5-6s so the audience can "digest" the destruction in front of them. A 远景 of ruins is not a transition, it is the narrative itself
- **Survival action at normal rhythm** — action shots such as searching for supplies, walking and keeping watch run 2-4s, holding the rhythm that keeps the narrative moving
- **Compress the crisis shots** — shots of a threat appearing, of flight, of combat are compressed to 1.5-2s; the fast rhythm carries the urgency
- **Give a moral choice enough time** — the decision shot in a moral dilemma (leave the wounded or not, shoot or not, share the food or not) gets 4-5s so the audience suffers through it alongside the character
- **The golden 6-second rule** — apart from 远景 shots of ruins and one-take exploration, a single shot does not exceed 6s
- **Air in the silent wasteland** — a wasteland-walking passage may run 3-4 consecutive shots with 无台词 (no dialogue) and no scoring, pure ambience plus footsteps. The total silence does not exceed 20s, but it is enough for the audience to feel "the emptiness of the world"

---

## 5. Designing survival action

- **Suspense in the search shots** — the storyboard logic of a supply-search passage: 全景 (wide shot) sweeping the space → 中景 approaching the target → 特写 examining the object → the result (found / empty-handed). Every search carries the suspense of "is there anything?"
- **The chaos of combat and flight** — post-apocalyptic combat is not an exchange of moves; it is chaotic, undignified, whatever-it-takes. The storyboard carries the real chaos of staying alive through handheld tracking + fast cutting + imperfect composition (half the frame blocked)
- **The moral weight of fighting over resources** — when fighting other survivors for supplies, the storyboard must not only shoot the "action" but the "eyes": the other person's fear, the hesitation in one's own hand, a 特写 of the supplies being torn out of the other's grip. Let the audience feel the moral cost of every fight
- **Exploration in a long take** — entering a new ruined space can use a one-take tracking shot (the character pushes the door → walks in → looks around → discovers something) running up to 8-10s, feeding new spatial information continuously to hold the tension
- **Do not fragment what one shot can state** — one tracking long take (the character searches the room → finds the object → puts it in the pack) is more immersive than breaking it into three fragments

---

## 6. Narrating the moral choice

- **Use silence for the moral dilemma** — strip out all scoring in passages facing a moral choice (save or not, kill or not, share or not), leaving only ambience plus the character's breathing. Let the weight of the silence press down on the audience
- **Three beats for the choice shot** — (1) the 全景 facing the dilemma (the situation) → (2) a 近景 (medium close-up) or 特写 on the character's face (the struggle) → (3) the result of the action (the choice). Done in three beats, no dragging
- **Trust and betrayal** — shoot the moment of betrayal in "revealing" shot language: pull slowly back from the point of view of the one who trusts, revealing the betrayer's act → cut to a 特写 of the trusting one's face. The fact first, the reaction second
- **The ritual of sacrifice** — give a character's self-sacrifice its full ritual: the last look exchanged (a shot-reverse-shot in 近景) → turning and walking towards the danger (a 全景 of the back) → the reaction of those left behind (特写). Do not rush past it; a sacrifice deserves to be gazed at
- **The glimmer of goodness** — for shots where someone chooses goodness inside despair (giving away the last of the food, risking themselves for a stranger), use a warm shift in the lighting plus a faint warm score easing in. But do not over-sentimentalise — the restraint is where the power is

---

## 7. Dialogue and air

- **Spare with words** — people in the post-apocalypse have no energy for small talk. Dialogue should be minimal and practical: "safe", "move", "how much further". The occasional line carrying feeling ("thank you for not leaving") lands with unusual force because it is rare
- **Environmental narrative instead of dialogue** — do not use dialogue to explain "what happened". Let the audience read the answer out of the ruins themselves: claw marks on a wall, a motorway jammed with dead cars, a wall covered in missing-person notices
- **Fragments of memory** — dialogue recalling "before the end" should be extremely short, flashing past like a fragment ("it was still sunny that day…" and then silence). Pair it with a 近景 plus a defocus or flashback treatment
- **One line, one shot** — every line in the post-apocalypse carries weight, so give it its own shot. Especially lines expressing trust, a promise or a farewell
- **Inner monologue over 远景** — a character's rare inner monologue is laid over a 远景 or an empty wasteland shot, the voice sounding as though it drifts in from "somewhere in this empty world"

---

## 8. Transition design

- **Hard cuts within a scene** — cut hard between shots inside one space to keep the narrative tight
- **Ruin montage** — when changing scene, stack 2-3 empty shots of ruins quickly (1-1.5s each), stating the geographic move while reinforcing the post-apocalyptic atmosphere
- **The signal-interference transition** — put an electronic interference effect on the image (static, banding, distortion) as the transition, implying the last gasp of a technological civilisation
- **Decay over time** — express the passing of time with contrasting shots of the same location at different degrees of decay (intact → rusted → collapsed); it suits transitions between memory and the present
- **Flashing back to civilisation** — insert a 1-2s short flashback to an image from "before the end" (a busy street, a laughing crowd) to form a stinging contrast with the present ruins. The flashback image should be brighter and warmer in texture, forming a colour-temperature contrast with the grey present
- **Walking as the transition between passages** — a wasteland-walking passage is the natural passage divider of post-apocalyptic narrative; give it 2-3 远景 shots to state the move from A to B
