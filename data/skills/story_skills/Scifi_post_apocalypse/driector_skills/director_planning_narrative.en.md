# Narrative technique · Sci-Fi Post-Apocalypse · technique reference

---

## 1. Theme, premise and the post-apocalyptic core

### Key points for sci-fi post-apocalypse narrative

- **Survival is the operating system underneath the narrative** — in the post-apocalypse every act (walking, drinking, sleeping, trusting) is redefined by the premise of "stay alive". Every decision in the narrative should be weighed against its survival cost
- **The wreckage of civilisation is narrative** — the traces of civilisation left in the ruins (a family photograph, a rusted fairground ride, an abandoned school bus) carry narrative force by themselves — they say without words "there was something here once". Use environmental narrative instead of explaining through dialogue
- **Human nature shows itself at the extreme** — the post-apocalypse is a natural "laboratory of human nature": when there is not enough to go round, who is abandoned first? At the edge of death, who chooses to sacrifice? When betrayal buys a way out, who wavers? The extreme environment magnifies what people normally hide until there is nowhere left to hide it
- **Suggested directions for the exit feeling** — desolate and overwhelming / finding a way out of the dead end / a reckoning with human nature / bitter hope. Avoid directions that do not match the post-apocalyptic temperament, such as "light-hearted", "humorous" or "sweet"
- **The weight of hope is proportional to the despair** — hope in the post-apocalypse must not come cheap. The blacker the world, the more precious a sliver of light — a green shoot growing out of the ruins, a hand held out in the middle of despair, distant human voices heard while dying. Hope must be painted over a ground of despair

## 2. Narrative structure and survival rhythm

### Key points for sci-fi post-apocalypse narrative

- **Crisis drives the narrative** — the engine of post-apocalyptic narrative is not "what do I want" but "I must live through today". Every passage should carry a clear survival crisis (supplies running out, a threat closing in, the shelter falling, a companion wounded); the crisis drives the action and the action drives the narrative
- **Resources are the conflict** — food, water, medicine, ammunition, a safe place to rest — scarcity is the root of post-apocalyptic conflict. The narrative should keep the audience feeling "there is not enough" at all times
- **Trust is tested in stages** — trust in the post-apocalypse is scarcer than supplies. The trust line follows the progression "probe → cooperate → test → betray or hold". The stakes of each test of trust should be higher than the last
- **Use the wasteland as a breath between passages** — put low-intensity passages between the crises (walking the wasteland, exploring ruins) as emotional buffers. These passages are not "empty"; they fill in the world through environmental narrative
- **The hope curve** — the emotion of post-apocalyptic narrative does not fall continuously; it runs a sawtooth of "despair → a glimmer → deeper despair → a stronger glimmer". After each bottom, give a sliver of hope (finding a water source, meeting someone kind, discovering a shelter), then overturn that hope with a heavier blow
- **The ending need not be whole** — the best ending for a post-apocalyptic narrative is not "everything returns to normal" but "walking on, carrying the scars". Surviving is itself the victory; the world does not have to be redeemed

## 3. Per-scene atmosphere design

### Key points for sci-fi post-apocalypse narrative

- **Describe atmosphere through decay** — do not say "desolate", say "the rust has crept up to the third step, the weeds are forcing their way out of the cracked floor tiles". A concrete detail of decay hits harder than an abstract word
- **Typical post-apocalyptic passages and their design** —

| Passage type | Emotional direction | Narrative technique | Music suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exploring the ruins | Alert + curious | Advancing slowly, sweeping the space, finding the relics of civilisation | A low-frequency hum + occasional clanging metal |
| Fighting over resources | Urgent + confrontational | Searching for supplies, fighting others for them, making trade-offs | Rhythmic noise floor, building |
| Rest stop / camp | Exhausted + fragile | Round the fire, a simple shared meal, a brief human warmth | A guitar or harmonica solo at very low volume |
| Crisis / threat | Fear + survival | Pursued, the shelter falling, a companion wounded | A heartbeat drum + a piercing alarm, or total silence |
| A choice about human nature | Struggle + weight | Whether to abandon the wounded, whether to open the last tin | Scoring disappears, pure silence |
| A glimmer of hope | Bitter + warm | A green shoot found in the ruins, voices in the distance, sunrise | A single warm instrument fading in out of the silence |

- **Decay in space is narrative about time** — how far the environment has decayed states directly "how long ago the end came":
  - **Recent**: buildings largely intact but empty of people, belongings scattered as though everyone just left
  - **Middle**: rust, weeds everywhere, partial collapse, nature beginning to take the territory back
  - **Distant**: ruins, broken walls, vegetation over everything, only the outline of civilisation left
- **The environment is the threat** — in the post-apocalypse the environment itself is the enemy: poisoned air, unstable buildings, contaminated water, extreme weather. The storyboard should keep the audience feeling the hostility of the environment at all times
- **Write the shot intent as "the feeling of survival", not "a description of the scene"** — "the 远景 (long shot) is there so the audience feels how small and helpless a person is in ruins without end" beats "shoot the ruins in 远景"

## 4. Sound and music direction

### Key points for sci-fi post-apocalypse narrative

- **Silence is the ground colour of the post-apocalypse** — the most unsettling thing in the post-apocalypse is not a monster's roar but "no sound where there should be sound": a city empty of people, a factory that no longer runs, a silent fairground. The silence itself narrates the dying out
- **Industrial waste noise** — metal scraping, a pipe venting, the crackle of a shorting cable, an unidentified rumble far off — these "sounds of civilisation's remains" make up the distinctive soundscape of the post-apocalypse
- **Extreme restraint in scoring** — total scoring coverage across the film does not exceed 30%. The power of the post-apocalypse is in the sense of vacuum, and too much scoring weakens the impact of the barrenness
- **The narrative function of alarms and signal tones** — the occasional sound from surviving electronics (a broadcast with no signal, an emergency message on a loop, an intermittent beep) is the "ghost voice" peculiar to the post-apocalypse and carries enormous atmosphere
- **Music-emotion model** —

| Emotional stage | Music strategy | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Exploring the ruins | A low-frequency hum + occasional metal | Low |
| Everyday survival | No scoring, pure ambience | None |
| The crisis closes in | A heartbeat drum, building | Medium |
| A choice about human nature | Complete silence | None |
| Combat / flight | Rhythmic electronic noise floor + percussion | Medium-high |
| A glimmer of hope | A single warm instrument easing in | Low→medium |

- **One sound sets the atmosphere** — in the post-apocalypse a single sudden sound hits harder than a rich soundscape: one gunshot in the distance, the creak of a door blown open by the wind, a cry for help from under the rubble. In complete silence, one sound is one event

## 5. Composition and shot-size narrative

### Key points for sci-fi post-apocalypse narrative

- **The narrative function of the three core compositions** —
  - **Ruins in 全景** — a tiny human figure set inside a vast field of ruins, carrying the post-apocalyptic sense of "civilisation is gone, people are ants". It is the most frequently used composition in sci-fi post-apocalypse
  - **Oppressive space** — moving through low ceilings, narrow passages, collapsed ruins; the physical pressure of the space carries the suffocation of survival directly
  - **Person against ruins** — set the character beside the relics of civilisation (standing before an abandoned skyscraper, walking an abandoned motorway), narrating the fall of civilisation through the contrast of scale
- **The "last person alive" feel of centred composition** — in the post-apocalypse, centred composition implies the extreme loneliness of "you are the only one left in these ruins"; save it for the signature shot of a lone traveller in the ruins
- **The disorder of tilted composition** — the Dutch angle in the post-apocalypse carries the psychological implication that "the world has already tilted, order is gone"; it suits the early disaster, a mental collapse, or a passage where the environment is extremely dangerous
- **Foreground blocking with ruins** — use wreckage as a foreground mass over the subject, creating the peeping or searching feel of "glimpsing a survivor through the ruins"
- **Shot size is the state of survival** —
  - **大远景 (extreme long shot) / 远景** — the whole of the ruins, wasteland without end, the macro shock of a civilisation gone
  - **全景 (wide shot)** — where the survivor stands in the space, where the threat is
  - **中景 (medium shot)** — acts of survival (searching, keeping watch, walking), interaction between characters
  - **特写 (close-up)** — the details of survival (cracked lips, a hand covered in dust, the last tin of food), emotional focus
- **The "relic" function of 大特写** — in the post-apocalypse 大特写 (extreme close-up) usually focuses on a "relic of civilisation": a faded family photograph, a dead phone screen, a doll covered in dust. Such details carry a thousand words of narrative on their own

## 6. Camera movement and rhythm

### Key points for sci-fi post-apocalypse narrative

- **Handheld is the base language of the post-apocalypse** — 50% or more of the shots use a slight handheld, carrying the survival anxiety of "unstable, anything could happen at any moment". Grade the amplitude: a small shake when safe → a clear shake when in danger → violent in combat
- **Slow move for exploration** — the ruin-exploration passages use an extremely slow lateral move, simulating a character advancing with great care. The slower it goes, the tenser the audience gets ("what will I see the next second?")
- **Fast push in = the crisis breaks** — when a threat appears suddenly, push in hard and fast to simulate the physical reaction of being startled or hit
- **Slow pull back = revealing the barrenness** — pulling slowly from a person or a detail out to 远景 reveals the ruins around them going on without end. It is the classic "sigh move" of post-apocalyptic narrative
- **Passages shot without a stabiliser** — deliberately drop the stabiliser for flight and combat passages, using real image shake to carry the chaos and the fear
- **Camera movement is the state of survival** — 静止 (static) = safe / on watch; slow move = exploring / searching; handheld tracking = travelling / uneasy; fast push in = crisis / discovery; slow pull back = despair / loneliness
- **360° sweep of the ruins** — when a character reaches a new place, use a slow 360° sweep so the audience "scans" the surroundings along with the character and judges how safe it is. It serves as establishing and as suspense at once
