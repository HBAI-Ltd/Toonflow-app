# Narrative technique · Historical Epic · technique reference

---

## 1. Theme, premise and the emotional core

### Key points for historical-epic narrative

- **A great age, small people** — the core of epic narrative is not re-enacting historical events, it is showing the fate of the individual inside the flood of an age. The theme and premise should focus on "how one person's choice refracts a whole era"
- **Fate as an irresistible force** — the tension of historical narrative comes from "the collision between individual will and the flood of the age". The harder a character struggles against it, the clearer the cruelty of fate and the greatness of human nature become
- **Ritual is weight** — the key historical beats (an enthronement, an oath, a decisive battle, a final parting, a martyrdom) must be given the feel of ritual. Ritual is not a big set piece; it is that every gesture and every line carries the gravity of "there is no going back"
- **Suggested directions for the feeling on leaving** — desolation / grandeur / rueful sighing / awe. Avoid flippant directions such as "satisfying" or "getting even"
- **The echo of history** — good epic narrative makes the audience feel "this did not happen only in that era". Use the dilemmas of human nature that run across time (loyalty against righteousness, the individual against the collective) to build resonance across eras

## 2. Narrative structure and rhythm planning

### Key points for historical-epic narrative

- **Interweaving the macro and the micro is the basic craft** — epic narrative must switch constantly between "the macro 全景 (wide shot)" and "the micro individual". Pure macro is hollow; pure micro loses the epic. Every grand passage needs an individual emotional anchor
- **The epic rhythm arc** — the rhythm of an epic does not advance at a constant speed, it is a long wave of "accumulate → erupt → settle". A long build-up and accumulation buys one shattering eruption, and then silence digests the aftershock
- **Ensemble narrative** — an epic usually carries several character lines. Each line should have its own emotional arc, and they converge at the key beats. The point of convergence is the climax of the epic
- **Handling a span of time** — historical narrative often spans years or decades. Convey the passage of time with "events that mark time" rather than "date captions": white hair, new grass on the ruins, a child grown up
- **Turn on silence, not on a speech** — a historical turning point should be handled by silent means first (an exchanged look, sitting alone, gazing into the distance) rather than by impassioned oratory. Real historical choices are usually made in silence
- **The "last time" model** — the tear-jerking beats of epic narrative are usually a "last time": the last look back at the home city, the last cup raised, the last battle fought side by side. Mark each character's "last time" beat in the narrative plan

## 3. Per-scene emotional design

### Key points for historical-epic narrative

- **Write the emotional target in epic vocabulary** — do not say "sad", say "the desolation of watching an era come down". Epic emotion has to step out of the personal level and carry the sense of an era and of fate
- **Typical emotional passages and their design** —

| Passage type | Emotional direction | Narrative technique | Music suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Times of peace and plenty | Prosperity + undercurrent | Show order and splendour, and plant the seeds of the collapse to come | Solemn instrumental, an undercurrent moving beneath |
| The world overturns | Shock + powerlessness | The chain reaction of order collapsing, the individual swept along | Sudden silence, then low-frequency pressure |
| The choice in the trap | Torment + resolve | The character struggles between loyalty and righteousness, life and death, and finally makes the choice | A single instrument solo, extremely spare |
| Decisive battle / standoff | Valour + grief | Doing what is known to be impossible, stating resolve through action rather than dialogue | From silence to the instruments filling out |
| Price / sacrifice | Pain + awe | The price of victory, the last moments of the one sacrificed | Choral voices or pure ambient sound |
| The dust settles | Desolation + release | The silence after everything ends, the survivor looking back | Extremely spare instrumental, or wind |

- **"The space of power" is the core narrative tool** — map power relations and the standing of a fate through spatial relations:
  - **High ground**: looking down = holding power / passing judgement; suits emperors and decision-makers
  - **Low ground**: looking up = submission / smallness; suits the individual at the mercy of fate
  - **Eye level**: equal footing / standoff; suits the key dialogue between evenly matched parties
- **Space is the era** — convey the turning of an era through the change of a space: a once-splendid hall become ruins, a former battlefield grown over with weeds, an empty throne. Space is itself the most powerful narrative of an era
- **What a ritual scene requires of the narrative** — in a ritual passage (a coronation, an oath, a final parting) every gesture must carry narrative meaning: kneeling = submission and sacrifice; raising the cup = a pact of life and death; turning away = no going back. Do not let it slide into empty pageantry

## 4. Sound and music direction

### Key points for historical-epic narrative

- **A heavy low end is the base colour of an epic** — the foundation of an epic score is the weight of the low register (cello, timpani, low horns). Keep the high register for the key emotional eruptions; the everyday passages hold up "the weight of history" on the low end
- **The power of the human voice** — in epic narrative the human voice (chorus, chant, work song) has a special narrative function: the war song of an army marching out, the elegy of a farewell, the silent chorus after victory. The voice carries "the fate of a people" better than instruments do
- **The solemnity of silence** — at the key historical instants (the last decision on the throne, the exchanged look before an execution, the moment the shooting stops on the battlefield) remove all score and leave only ambient sound or pure silence. Silence gives those instants the weight of "history is happening"
- **Ambient sound carries the texture of the era** — every scene should mark its core ambient sound to convey the atmosphere of the era: iron hooves, banners snapping, a distant horn, the din of the marketplace, wind in the ruins
- **Model for the progression of musical emotion** —

| Emotional stage | Music strategy | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Build-up / everyday | Low instruments under the scene, solemn but restrained | Medium-low |
| Undercurrent / accumulation | Drums entering, the rhythm hinting that fate is closing in | Medium |
| Eruption / decisive battle | Full instrumental, or sudden total silence | Extreme |
| Sacrifice / price | Choral voices or a single instrument keening | Medium-high |
| The dust settles | Extremely spare instrumental, or pure ambient sound (wind) | Low |

- **A battlefield is not noise** — the sound design of a combat passage should not be the noise of a chaotic melee, it should be a layered soundscape: breathing and the clash of weapons close in, the shouts of killing at 中景 (medium shot) distance, horns and hooves far off. Layering is narrative

## 5. Composition and shot-size narrative

### Key points for historical-epic narrative

- **The narrative function of the three core compositions** —
  - **Symmetrical composition** — solemnity / power / ritual / an order that cannot be defied; suits the court, the great hall, a standoff
  - **Rule of thirds** — everyday life, marching, dialogue; stable and balanced, suits the passages that carry the narrative forward
  - **Large areas of empty space** — desolation / smallness / the emptiness of fate, the loneliness of one person between heaven and earth
- **The epic value of the macro 远景** — 远景 (long shot) and 大远景 are the workhorse shot sizes of epic narrative. A 远景 is not a transition, it is "letting the audience feel the breadth of an era and the smallness of an individual". Give the 远景 ample duration
- **特写 is the anchor of humanity** — cutting from a grand 远景 into a 特写 (close-up) of one person's eyes is the core technique of "coming back from the era to the person". The greater the contrast of 远景 → 特写, the stronger the emotional impact
- **Depth in space is power** — position in the depth of the frame implies the power relation: the person standing furthest back holds the most power (stronger still with their back to the camera); the person kneeling in 近景 (medium close-up) is the most abject
- **Ensemble composition** — an epic often needs several characters in one frame. The core of ensemble composition is "making the individual stand out within the group" — use position, posture and the contrast of movement against stillness so the main character is recognisable in the crowd
- **The lone individual and the vast space** — one person standing in an empty battlefield, in ruins, in a great hall: the smaller the figure's proportion in the frame, the greater the loneliness and the weight of history

## 6. Camera movement and rhythm

### Key points for historical-epic narrative

- **Solemn slow moves by default** — camera movement in epic narrative should proceed at an extremely slow speed, conveying "the heavy footsteps of history". A fast push, a fast cut and other hurried devices are incompatible with the epic temperament
- **Looking down from above = the point of view of fate** — a high-angle overhead view is the signature camera movement of an epic, implying "fate, or history, looking down on all living things". Suits a 全景 (wide shot) of a battlefield, a city falling, a tide of people on the move
- **Low angle = reverence / oppression** — a low-angle shot suits a hero's last stand, a monument, a city wall. The steeper the low angle, the stronger the solemnity or the oppression
- **Gaze without moving** — at a key historical instant (a final parting, an enthronement, the moment before the last battle) the camera should be absolutely 静止 (static), like a witness of history looking on. Any camera movement breaks the solemnity of "time frozen"
- **Slow pan = showing the whole picture / the scroll of an era** — a slow horizontal pan suits showing a large-scale scene: an army massing, the full sweep of a city, the ruins after a battle. The speed of a slow pan should give the audience time to "read" every detail in the frame
- **Camera movement is narrative point of view** — looking down from above = the point of view of fate (cool, omniscient); lateral tracking = walking with the character (empathy); 缓推 (slow push in) = focusing on what matters (emphasis); a slow pull back = revealing the whole picture (awe)
