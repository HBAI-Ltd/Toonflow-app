# Narrative technique · Psychological Drama · technique reference

---

## 1. Theme, premise and the emotional core

### Key points for psychological drama narrative

- **Calm on the surface, currents underneath** — the core appeal of psychological drama is that the inside does not match the outside. Every apparently ordinary line has a second meaning, every smile may be a carefully calculated move. The theme and premise should turn on "the invisible war of attack and defence between people"
- **Information is the weapon** — the engine underneath psychological-drama narrative is asymmetric information. Who knows what, who does not, who is pretending not to — the hiding, guessing and revealing of information is the core of the narrative tension
- **A layered game of knowing** — the highest form of psychological drama is not simple deception but nested knowing: A thinks he is deceiving B → B has known all along → A knows that B knows but pretends not to. The more layers, the greater the intellectual pleasure for the audience
- **Suggested directions for the exit feeling** — mind-bending / a chill down the back / sudden understanding / blood running cold. Avoid purely sensory directions such as "gore" or "violence"
- **The grey of human nature** — there are no absolute good or bad people in psychological-drama narrative. Every character has their own logic, their own reasons and their own limit. The best game is one where "the audience understands every side's motive and does not know which side to be on"

## 2. Narrative structure and rhythm planning

### Key points for psychological drama narrative

- **Controlling information is the basic craft** — the core task of psychological-drama narrative is controlling the flow of information. The narrative-planning stage must state, for every passage: what the audience knows, what character A knows, what character B knows. The gaps between those three are the source of the tension
- **Switching point of view flips understanding** — present the same event from different characters' points of view, and every switch flips the audience's reading of the event once more. The plan should mark the "point-of-view switch points" and the update in understanding each switch brings
- **A reversal needs setup** — every reversal must have enough clues buried ahead of it. At the reversal the audience should feel "I should have seen it!" rather than "that came out of nowhere". A suggested ratio of setup to reversal is 3:1 (three parts of setup buy one reversal)
- **Tension builds → brief release → greater tension** — the rhythm of psychological drama spirals upward: after each round of the game there is a brief slackening, but the stakes of the next round are higher and the risk greater
- **The "exposure" rhythm model** — the core rhythm of psychological drama is the step-by-step exposure of the characters' hidden cards: disguise → a crack appears → the other side notices → a counter-move → a deeper disguise → a greater exposure. Every round comes closer to the truth than the last
- **Designing where the audience stands** — state for each passage whether the audience has the "god's-eye view" (knowing both sides' cards) or the "following view" (knowing only one side's information). The two positions produce completely different tension: the god's-eye view = the anxiety of watching a character walk into a trap; the following view = the shock of being deceived alongside the character

## 3. Per-scene emotion design

### Key points for psychological drama narrative

- **State the emotional target in the vocabulary of the game** — do not say "tense", say "talking with a smile while the palms are soaked". Do not say "afraid", say "he has realised the other side knows, but not how much they know". The emotion of the game is always layered and contradictory
- **Typical emotional passages and their design** —

| Passage type | Emotional direction | Narrative technique | Music suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peace on the surface | Politeness + undercurrent | Normal conversation in which every line carries a second meaning | An extremely light bed or no scoring, letting the tension of the dialogue work on its own |
| Probing exchange | Tension + intellectual pleasure | Attack and defence hidden in the back and forth, words within words | A low-frequency undercurrent, the rhythm subtly accelerating |
| A crack appears | Unease + alertness | Some detail has given something away, a flicker of change on the other side's face | A dissonant interval flashes past, then calm returns |
| Showdown / reversal | Shock + realisation | The cards are turned over and every earlier clue suddenly links up | Sudden silence, or a single sustained low note |
| Deadlocked standoff | Suffocation + balance | Both sides know that the other knows, and neither moves first | Completely silent, only ambience and breathing |
| Final revelation | Overturned + rueful | The final truth runs one layer deeper than anyone assumed | Minimal instrumentation, or a lingering resonance |

- **"Dialogue is the battlefield" is the core narrative tool** — in psychological drama, dialogue is not the transfer of information but a weapon of attack and defence:
  - **Asking the question** = probing / attack (trying to draw information out)
  - **Evading or changing the subject** = defence (not wanting to expose information)
  - **Answering with a question** = counter-attack (pushing the pressure back onto the other side)
  - **Silence** = the strongest card (letting the other side expose themselves through their own anxiety)
- **Space is the field of power** — in a psychological-drama scene, whoever holds the commanding position in the space holds the upper hand in the game: sitting at the other end of the table = an even contest; standing up and moving about = applying pressure; turning your back on the other side = confidence or contempt; trapped in a corner = the losing position
- **The micro-expression tells the truth** — the advanced technique of psychological-drama narrative is "the mouth lies while the body tells the truth". In the narrative plan, every game passage should mark the character's "real reaction" (micro-expressions, unconscious small gestures); these are the clues given to the audience

## 4. Sound and music direction

### Key points for psychological drama narrative

- **Silence is the strongest weapon** — the key standoff passages of a psychological drama should have the scoring stripped out first, leaving only ambience and the characters' breathing and heartbeats. Dialogue inside silence is more tense than dialogue under any score
- **Scoring implants a reading** — the score in a psychological drama is not there to swell the atmosphere but to steer the audience's judgement. The score under a character's entrance implies "there is something wrong with this person"; an uneasy low note under a calm conversation implies "there is a trap"
- **Sound can deceive** — the score can work with the narrative to "fool" the audience: warm scoring makes the audience assume a character is good, and at the reversal the change of key in the score is itself the narrative device that says "ah, so that is how it is"
- **Ambience is psychological space** — the echo of an enclosed space = trapped / nowhere to run; a conversation in a noisy environment = secret, not safe to speak plainly; an empty, quiet space = nowhere to hide, everything is heard
- **Model for the progression of musical emotion** —

| Emotional stage | Music strategy | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Peace on the surface | An extremely light bed or no scoring | Very low |
| Currents underneath | Low-frequency dissonance, volume rising | Medium-low |
| The exchange escalates | Rhythmic implication, the tension raised | Medium |
| Showdown / reversal | Sudden silence, or silence after one strong hit | Extreme |
| Endgame | Minimal instrumentation, a resonance left behind | Low |

- **"The exposing heartbeat"** — in the key game passages, use the character's heartbeat as a sound effect. An accelerating heartbeat implies the character is lying, tense, or about to be exposed. But use it with restraint — turning it into the character's "lie detector" reduces the suspense

## 5. Composition and shot-size narrative

### Key points for psychological drama narrative

- **The narrative function of the three core compositions** —
  - **Symmetrical composition** — two people seated opposite or facing off, carrying "evenly matched / standoff / the game in balance"
  - **Triangular composition** — the third party in a three-way game (observer, variable, arbiter), breaking the binary opposition and adding complexity
  - **Unbalanced composition** — the subject pushed to one side with empty space or a threatening element on the other, implying "the balance has broken / someone is in the dark"
- **Shot size is power over information** — 特写 (close-up) = the audience is allowed to see the micro-expression (mind-reading); 中景 (medium shot) = the audience can only see body language (guessing); 远景 (long shot) = the audience can only see positional relations (reading the layout). Use shot size to control the audience's "mind-reading permission"
- **The attack and defence of dialogue shot sizes** — the shot sizes in a shot-reverse-shot should change with the state of the game: give the side with the upper hand 近景 (medium close-up) (confident, composed); give the losing side 特写 (tense, micro-expression exposed); when the sides are evenly matched, a two-person 中景
- **Depth of space is the layout of the game** — use the depth of the space to carry the relationships in the game: foreground = the current speaker (what is stated); middle ground = the listener (observing, calculating); background = the third party or the hidden information
- **The mind-reading function of 大特写** — 大特写 (extreme close-up) (pupil, corner of the mouth, fingertip) is for catching a character's "tell while lying" or "the flicker of change on realising they have been fooled". These are the clues given to the audience and must be exact
- **"What was not meant to be seen"** — the advanced compositional technique of game narrative: let the audience see, "by accident", through the composition, what a character is trying to hide (the hand shaking behind the back, the fist clenched under the table, the momentary drift of the eyes)

## 6. Camera movement and rhythm

### Key points for psychological drama narrative

- **The standoff passage is absolutely 静止 (static)** — in the core standoff passages of psychological drama the camera should be as completely 静止 as a held breath. 60% or more of the shots are on a 静止 camera. Any camera movement pulls the audience's attention away from the micro-expressions and the subtext of the dialogue
- **缓推 = closing in on the truth / applying pressure** — an extremely slow push in implies "the audience or the character is closing in on the truth" or "one side is applying pressure to the other". The slower it goes, the stronger the pressure
- **Slow pull back = revealing the whole / discovery** — pulling slowly back (拉远) from a detail reveals the whole that the character has not noticed (someone listening from the dark, the evidence right behind them); it is the classic device for "the audience knows and the character does not"
- **The subtle drift** — inside a 静止 frame the camera drifts extremely slowly to one side, implying "the scales of the game are tipping". Whoever it drifts towards is the one losing the advantage
- **Fast cutting for the instant of the reversal** — the only moment fast cutting is permitted in psychological drama is the instant the cards are turned over: a fast jump through shot sizes (全景 → 特写 → 大特写) simulating the psychological impact of sudden understanding
- **Camera movement is psychological posture** — 静止 = standoff / balance; 缓推 = closing in / applying pressure; slow pull back = revelation / giving ground; drift = the scales tipping; fast cutting = the truth exposed
