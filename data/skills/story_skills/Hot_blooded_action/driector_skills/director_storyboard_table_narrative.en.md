# Storyboard-table narrative technique · Hot-Blooded Youth · technique reference

---

## 1. What the storyboard table is for

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into shot language. The fields of the form are set by the director as the project requires (shot number, shot size, camera movement, duration, characters, events, dialogue, lighting, emotion, transitions and so on); what follows is only a technique reference for the hot-blooded-youth narrative genre.

---

## 2. Choosing the shot size

- **Shot size is the expression of force** — the core logic of shot size in a hot-blooded storyboard is "carrying the sense of force and the intensity of the emotion". The low point uses a high-angle 远景 (long shot) (smallness); the eruption uses a low-angle 特写 (close-up) (force)
- **The low angle is the core weapon** — a low angle carries its own psychological cue of "tall, unshakeable". The low angle is mandatory when the protagonist stands up, declares a conviction, throws the final blow
- **The high angle makes the dead end** — a high angle makes the subject look small and helpless. Use a high angle when the protagonist is beaten to the ground and when facing an overwhelming enemy, so it forms the maximum contrast with the low angle that follows
- **大特写 is reserved for the threshold instants** — 大特写 (extreme close-up) — a pupil, a fist, a drop of blood — is an emotional nuclear weapon; use it 1-2 times per passage. Place it at the last moment before the eruption to detonate the emotion
- **The establishing shot must have pressure** — a hot-blooded establishing shot does not only state the environment, it must carry pressure through the space: a huge arena, endless ruins, the towering figure of the enemy. Get it done in 1-2 shots; a 远景 is enough

---

## 3. Camera-movement rhythm

- **Gathering passages are mostly still** — the gathering passages before the eruption run a 70% 静止 (static) camera, letting silence and expression accumulate the emotion
- **Eruption passages release the kinetic energy** — at the instant of the blow or the awakening, switch to moving shots such as a fast push, tracking or a lateral move, releasing the energy that was gathered
- **Fast push = the impact of striking out** — pushing hard towards the subject, imitating the release of force. Used on the first frame of a blow, on the instant the eyes open on awakening
- **环绕 = the hero freeze-frame** — a slow 环绕 (orbit) around the protagonist, at most 1-2 times in the whole piece, for the hottest "hero's moment"
- **Overcranked camera movement = time dilation** — shoot the key blow overcranked (slow motion), magnifying the force and the detail of the instant of impact. No more than 2 overcranked shots per passage
- **Camera movement is the direction of force** — push = advance / attack; pull = gather / retreat; pan = sweeping the battlefield; follow = walking shoulder to shoulder; 环绕 = the hero's gaze

---

## 4. Duration control

- **Stretch the gathering shots** — give the shots before the eruption — "standing up", "clenching the fist", "opening the eyes" — 4-5s, or even 6s combined with overcranking, so the emotion accumulates fully
- **Compress the impact shots** — compress the instant of the blow, the collision or the hit to 1-1.5s (a flash cut); the sense of speed is the sense of impact
- **The golden moment of the overcrank** — an overcranked shot (slow motion) may exceed the 6s limit, but not 8s. Its information output is sustained by detail (flying debris, hair streaming, the ground shaking)
- **Everyday shots keep a normal rhythm** — hold tender and everyday passages to a normal 2-4s rhythm; do not use combat rhythm there, let the audience relax
- **The golden 6-second rule** — a non-overcranked shot does not exceed 6s. The "sense of force" in hot-blooded narrative does not come from dragging out duration, it comes from contrast in rhythm
- **The contrast-rhythm formula** — the last shot before the eruption (slow / long) → the first shot of the eruption (fast / short); the extreme contrast in duration delivers the maximum impact

---

## 5. Designing the combat shots

- **Alternate the rhythm of attack and defence** — the storyboard of a combat passage cannot be all attack: attack → defend → fall back → attack again; there is only tension if it goes both ways. A purely one-sided steamroller has no narrative
- **Show the power gap with shot size** — when the enemy is strong and we are weak: shoot the enemy in a low-angle 全景 (wide shot) (hugeness) and the protagonist in a high-angle 中景 (medium shot) (smallness). Reverse it when the tables turn
- **One blow decides it** — give the last blow of the ultimate duel a full ritual: gather (overcranked 特写) → strike (flash-cut fast push) → hit (大特写 + impact empty space) → result (slow pull back to 全景). Present all four beats
- **Being wounded is growth** — the protagonist's wounds need a 特写 in the storyboard: the drop of blood, the crack, the trembling hand. Pain is the "price" made visible, and the greater the price the more weight the victory carries
- **What one shot can state should not be split into two** — one long take with camera movement (for example tracking the protagonist from being driven back to charging again) hits harder than breaking it into fragmented shots. A combat long take may exceed 6s

---

## 6. Comrade narrative

- **Shoulder-to-shoulder composition** — when comrades share the frame use a horizontal composition, with the two on the same horizontal line of the frame, implying equality and trust
- **The shot that passes trust across** — one exchanged look, one wordless nod, one act of turning your back over to someone — trust needs no dialogue. Complete it with 近景 (medium close-up) cutting back and forth
- **The duration of a sacrifice shot** — give the shot of a comrade blocking the way or going down ample duration (4-5s), together with the reaction 特写 of the protagonist's face. Do not skate past it; this is a tear-jerking ignition beat
- **The ritual feel of the return shot** — the shot of comrades arriving at the critical moment: first the protagonist's despairing face → a sound or a shift of light from behind → a slow pull back revealing the comrades' figures. Do it in three beats, not in one step

---

## 7. Dialogue and the war cry

- **One line of conviction is enough** — the protagonist's declarations of conviction ("the people I have to protect are right behind me!") are pared down to 2-3 lines in the whole piece; do not mutter it over and over. One line with a low-angle 近景 pulls the force to full
- **Silence is stronger than a declaration** — use silence plus action instead of dialogue in the awakening and standing-up passages. The audience can read the conviction out of the action; the character does not need to say it
- **Restraint with the battle roar** — a pure "aaah——!" battle roar appears at most 2 times in the whole piece, placed at the instant of the ultimate eruption. Pair it with a fast push and a 大特写
- **The opponent's lines** — the opponent's mockery and doubt are a gathering tool: "you cannot possibly win", "just give up" — these lines are the fuel before the eruption; shoot the opponent saying them in 近景, then cut to a 特写 of the protagonist's silence
- **Narration is minimal** — hot-blooded narrative speaks through action; use narration only where an inner monologue must be supplied, over a 远景 or an empty shot

---

## 8. Transition design

- **Hard cut inside a fight** — shots inside a fight are joined by hard cuts, keeping the rhythm tight
- **Shockwave transition** — use a flash to white (0.3-0.5s) as the transition at the instant of a great technique or an eruption, imitating the visual effect of a shockwave
- **Black screen for gathering** — a short black screen (0.5-1s) may be used before the eruption as a "deep breath", and the picture that follows is the first frame of the eruption
- **Empty shots to breathe between passages** — between a combat passage and an everyday passage, use 1-2 ambient empty shots (the sky, the setting sun, falling petals) as an emotional bridge, giving the audience room to breathe
- **Flash cuts for memory flashbacks** — the images of conviction flashing through the protagonist's mind before the eruption (a comrade's smile, a promise to protect) are inserted as fast 0.5-1s flash cuts, without breaking the rhythm
