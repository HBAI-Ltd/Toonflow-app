# Medieval Epic — Director Narrative Skill Set

## Introduction

This skill set provides a complete director's narrative technique reference for the **Medieval Epic** genre — the wronged-hero medieval drama of 30–50 minute films: betrayal, exile, a single bond worth dying for, monster-hunting, and public vindication. It covers the whole pipeline from macro narrative planning to micro storyboard execution, and applies to any visual style.

## Core philosophy

- **A broken hero cast out by the system** — the story's engine is one person wronged by the secular powers that owned them (a hunters' guild, a lord, their own former army). The audience's contract is: *they were wrong about him or her, and one day everyone will see it.*
- **One bond worth dying for** — against the whole world stands a single connection: a dying girl, an elf, a hunted creature. The narrative's warmth lives entirely inside this bond; everything outside it is cold.
- **Public vindication is the payoff** — the genre's largest emotional payment is the moment the world admits its error: the army kneeling, the guild restoring the broken seal, the accuser's silence. Plan the whole film as the earning of this single moment (or its tragic mirror: vindication arriving after the sacrifice).
- **The wilderness is true, the hall lies** — moor, forest, and campfire are where honesty and the bond live; the guild hall and the lord's court are where power performs. Space itself carries the moral geography.

## Skill file descriptions

### 1. Narrative planning techniques (`director_planning_narrative.md`)

Director technique reference for the **narrative planning stage**: theme and emotional core, single-POV structure with percentage-based beats for 30–50 minute films, scene-level emotion design, secular ritual design (hunters' guild rites), sound and voice direction including UK-accent dialogue rules, and content-safe violence planning.

| Section | Content summary |
|---|---|
| Theme and emotional core | Wronged hero, the single bond, vindication contract, secular antagonists |
| Structure and pacing | Single POV, percentage beat table (30′/50′), middle-act expansion rule, the "last time" model |
| Scene emotion design | Eight canonical segments with music hints, power-space staging, guild rituals |
| Sound, music and voice | Low strings and silence, dialogue register, UK accent mapping, VO discipline |
| Composition and shot scale | Lone figure against the vast, the bond's two-shots, depth as power |
| Camera and rhythm | Slow solemn default, fate's-gaze aerials, duel and creature-reveal principles |

### 2. Storyboard-table narrative techniques (`director_storyboard_table_narrative.md`)

Director technique reference for the **storyboard-table stage**: shot-scale narration of power, solemn camera-movement norms, duration control, shot merging, long takes, confrontation and bond interaction design, dialogue and silence, transition logic, and safe violence staging.

| Section | Content summary |
|---|---|
| Table positioning | The storyboard table as the rhythm-control core |
| Shot-scale choice | Wide as the epic's breath, close-up density for short film, extreme close-ups for fate moments |
| Camera rhythm | ≥70% static or near-static, solemn movement vocabulary |
| Duration control | Wides 5–7s, dialogue 4–5s, ritual actions 3–5s each, close-ups 3–4s, long takes ≤15s |
| Shot merging and long takes | Group scenes in single moving masters, hunt sequences merged, ritual one-shots |
| Interaction, dialogue, transitions | Confrontation over combat, oath-short lines, silence as punctuation, empty-frame transitions |
| Safe violence staging | Impact off-frame, witness coverage, sparks not blood, death by stillness |
