# Narrative Techniques · Medieval Epic · Technique Reference

---

## 1. Theme and emotional core

### Essentials of the wronged-hero medieval epic

- **One hero, wronged by their own** — the protagonist is broken not by an enemy but by the system that owned them: the hunters' guild that stripped their seal, the lord they served, the army that called them traitor. The theme must answer: *what does this one person's endurance reveal about loyalty, and about the institutions that demand it?*
- **The audience contract: "they were wrong about him"** — from the first act the audience must know something the world refuses to see. Every scene of scorn and pursuit is a deposit; the vindication scene is the withdrawal. Never let the audience doubt the hero's core innocence — ambiguity belongs to the world, not to the contract
- **The single bond carries all warmth** — a dying girl to protect, an elf taken, a hunted creature that saved them. The bond must be: (a) weaker than the hero in power, (b) hunted or condemned by the same system, (c) the only character who sees the hero truly. All tenderness in the film routes through this bond
- **Secular antagonists only** — the opposing system is worldly: the hunters' guild council, a lord and his men-at-arms, the hero's former company, mercenary bands, bounty hunters. No religious institutions or symbolism anywhere in the story
- **The exit feeling to aim for** — earned release / grief with dignity / quiet awe. Avoid cheap triumph; even victory should taste of cost

## 2. Structure and pacing

### Essentials

- **Single POV, absolutely** — one protagonist's perspective carries the film. The antagonist system needs no POV scenes; it is seen only as the hero and the bond experience it. Sub-plots are forbidden at this length
- **Percentage beat sheet (30–50 minute frame)** — plan beats by proportion, converting per the target duration:

| Beat | Position | 30′ film | 50′ film |
|---|---|---|---|
| Betrayal / fall (seal stripped, condemned) | ≤ 20% | ≤ min 6 | ≤ min 10 |
| Meeting the bond | ~25% | ~min 8 | ~min 12 |
| Midpoint — exposure / loss (refuge found then broken) | ~50% | ~min 15 | ~min 25 |
| Second betrayal / the bond taken | ~65–70% | ~min 20 | ~min 33 |
| Vindication / sacrifice (the kneeling, the seal restored) | ~80–90% | min 24–27 | min 40–45 |
| Quiet ending (dust settles) | final 5–10% | 2–3 min | 3–5 min |

- **The middle expands, the ends do not** — a longer film grows only in act two: add hunt–refuge–exposure cycles and bond-deepening scenes. Act one's fall and act three's vindication keep their tempo at any length
- **The hunt–refuge–exposure cycle** — act two's engine: pursued (cold, moving) → shelter found (warm, still, bond deepens) → discovered (cold returns, stakes rise). Each cycle ends closer to the catastrophe; two cycles at 30′, three to four at 50′
- **Time passes through imagery** — a healed scar, snow on an old campsite, the bond walking unaided; never date subtitles
- **Turning points in silence** — the hero's key decisions (to protect, to return, to surrender) are made in wordless shots: a look, a turn, a sword picked back up
- **The "last time" model** — mark each character's "last time" node in the plan: the last shared fire, the last time the seal is touched, the last look back at the guild hall. These are the tear points

## 3. Scene-level emotion design

### Essentials

- **Write emotion goals in the genre's register** — not "sad", but "the shame of a loyal man made a traitor before his brothers". Emotion must carry institution and fate, not private mood alone
- **The eight canonical segments** —

| Segment | Emotional direction | Narrative technique | Music hint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Betrayal / judgment | Shock + shame | The rite of expulsion performed in full; the hero silent throughout | Sudden silence, then low strings |
| Exile / descent | Weariness + endurance | Cold travel, doors closed, the world's back turned | Sparse low strings, wind |
| Meeting the bond | Wariness + first warmth | Two hunted things circling; help given grudgingly | Single instrument enters shyly |
| Hunt–refuge cycle | Tension ↔ fragile peace | Alternate cold pursuit and firelight stillness | Drums under pursuit, hearth quiet in refuge |
| Bond deepening | Trust + fear of loss | Small acts of care; the hero begins protecting by choice | Instrument doubled, warmer |
| Second betrayal / taken | Fury + helplessness | The refuge violated; the bond seized; hero beaten not by force but by numbers | Full stop, then driving drums |
| Vindication / sacrifice | Awe + release | The truth undeniable before witnesses; the kneeling, or the sacrifice that forces it | Strings swell + distant single voice, then quiet |
| Dust settles | Desolation + peace | The survivor's quiet; the world changed but ordinary | Wind, embers, minimal strings |

- **Power space is a core tool** — map power through space: the guild's high seat looks down on the accused standing in the open circle; the lord speaks from horseback to those on foot; in the wilderness, the hero and the bond meet at eye level — the only equal space in the film
- **The guild's secular rituals give weight** — expulsion: the hunter's seal struck from the hero's hands, their blade broken before the council; wanted proclamation read on the village post; restoration: the seal returned in open hall; the kneeling: begun by one former brother, spreading in silence. Every rite is performed with full formality — the formality is what makes both the wound and the vindication land
- **Space is the moral map** — halls perform power; taverns leak truth; the moor strips pretense; the campfire circle is the only honest parliament. Returning spaces must register change: the hall that expelled him now kneels

## 4. Sound, music, and voice

### Essentials

- **Low and sparse is the base** — cello and viol at the bottom, frame drum for pursuit, war horn only at structural moments. Long stretches carry no music at all
- **The single voice over the choir** — one keening voice at sacrifice outweighs any choral mass; reserve choral texture for the vindication swell alone
- **Silence is the genre's organ** — the expulsion rite, the decision shots, the moment before the kneeling: total music silence, ambient only (wind, fire, mail rustle, crows)
- **Ambient carries the world** — mark 1–2 core ambient sounds per scene: wind over heather, fire crackle, rain on thatch, boots on stone, distant wolf
- **Dialogue register: words as oaths** — lines short and weighted, spoken as if carved: titles used in full ("Huntmaster", "my lord"), oaths and refusals in plain declaratives. One strong line beats a speech; key lines land into silence before and after
- **UK accent mapping (mandatory for dialogue and VO direction)** — Received Pronunciation for nobility and guild officers; regional UK accents (Northern English, Scottish, West Country) for soldiers, hunters, and commoners; the hero's accent marks their origin, not their rank — it stays common even at vindication. Delivery low and measured throughout
- **Voiceover discipline** — at most two VO moments: an opening line setting the world, a closing line sealing the meaning. Never VO to explain plot

## 5. Composition and shot-scale narration

### Essentials

- **The three core compositions** —
  - **Symmetry** — the hall, the council, the rite: power's order that must not be broken
  - **Rule of thirds** — travel, pursuit, tavern: the moving world
  - **Vast negative space** — the moor and the pass: one small figure against the indifferent world; the genre's signature frame
- **Wide shots are the epic's breath** — the lone rider on the moor, the hall's full height above the accused. Give wides generous duration; they carry "the world's scale against one person"
- **The close-up is the humanity anchor — and this genre's engine** — a 30–50 minute film lives on faces; close-up density runs higher than in long-form epic. After every vast wide, land on the eyes
- **The bond's two-shot** — the visual signature of the relationship: two figures sharing one frame at eye level, fire between or beside them. As trust grows, the two-shot tightens; after the taking, the hero is framed alone in the same setups
- **Depth is power** — the council deep in frame above; petitioners near and low; at the kneeling, the axis reverses: the hero deep and central, the kneeling crowd filling the foreground

## 6. Camera movement and rhythm

### Essentials

- **Default: slow and solemn** — the camera moves at the pace of consequence. Fast cutting and whip movement are alien to the genre except in the brief peak of pursuit
- **The fate's gaze** — high slow aerials over the moor, the hunt, the burning refuge: history looking down. Use at act turns
- **Low angle for the institution** — the hall, the high seat, the walls loom; the hero is looked down upon until vindication, when the axis levels
- **The held stare** — at rites and decisions, the camera locks still, a witness refusing to blink
- **Slow push as focus of fate** — onto the hero's face at the decision, onto the seal in the Huntmaster's hand
- **Duel principle** — escalate by scale change, not cut speed: wide (geography) → medium (bodies) → close (eyes) → insert (blades, sparks); keep each shot full length
- **Creature reveal principle** — part → silhouette → full presence under moon rim light; after the reveal, shoot the creature like a character: eyes first

## 7. Content-safe violence planning (mandatory)

> Downstream image and video models reject graphic-harm prompts, and bloodied character references poison every later generation. Violence is planned by implication from the narrative stage onward.

- **No scene may require a bloodied character** — wounds exist in story logic (the girl is dying, the hero is beaten) but are staged as fatigue, fever, supported walking, bandaged limbs unmarked by red — never open wounds or blood
- **Impact lands off-frame** — plan every strike toward off-screen; coverage is the witness, the swing, the aftermath
- **Death by stillness** — a hand releasing a sword, boots stopping, crows lifting, a riderless horse; the bond's or witness's face is the true death shot
- **Aftermath in metaphor** — torn banners, broken shields, grey snow, drifting smoke; never bodies in detail
- **Banned vocabulary in all downstream prompts** — blood, bleeding, gore, wound, stab, slash, execute, brutal, graphic, severed, corpse, torture; substitute: battle-worn, clash, sparks on impact, strike toward off-screen, fallen banner, empty helm, aftermath
