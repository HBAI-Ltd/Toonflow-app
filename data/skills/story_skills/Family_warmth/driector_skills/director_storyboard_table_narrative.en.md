# Storyboard-table narrative technique · Family Warmth · technique reference

---

## 1. What the storyboard table is for

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into shot language. The fields of the form are set by the director as the project requires (shot number, shot size, camera movement, duration, characters, events, dialogue, lighting, emotion, transitions and so on); what follows is only a technique reference for the family-warmth narrative genre.

---

## 2. Choosing the shot size

- **中景 is the workhorse of family narrative** — a large share of family scenes are "the family in one space"; 中景 (medium shot) and 全景 (wide shot) keep several people in frame and carry the belonging of "being together"
- **特写 catches the love that cannot be said** — much of the feeling in a family is not expressed in words. 特写 (close-up) is used to catch: the trembling hand, the eyes quietly gone red, the smile pressed down at the corner of the mouth, the extra piece of food quietly placed in someone's bowl
- **The keeping-watch function of 远景** — 远景 (long shot) in family narrative is mainly used for "watching someone go / waiting": the figure seeing them off at the door, the silhouette waiting at the window. Give 远景 enough duration (5-6s) for the emotion of "keeping watch" to come across fully
- **Save 大特写 for the moment feeling overflows** — 大特写 (extreme close-up) — eyes brimming / a hand clenched tight / trembling lips — is used only at the moment the feeling can no longer be held back; 2-3 times an episode is enough
- **Establish with a detail of daily life** — the establishing shot of family narrative does not use an empty architectural exterior, it uses a detail of daily life: a kitchen full of steam, slippers set straight beside the shoe cabinet, a note stuck on the fridge

---

## 3. Camera-movement rhythm

- **静止 by default; steady is warm** — 70% or more of the shots should be on a 静止 (static) camera. In family narrative 静止 carries the reassurance of "steady / unchanging / dependable"
- **缓推 (slow push in) = noticing a warm detail** — push slowly in on a warm detail (the meal left on the table, the shoes set out at the door, the blanket tucked back in) so the audience "discovers" that unspoken love
- **Slow pull back = leaving / watching them go** — back slowly away from the character; good for seeing someone off, for leaving home, for gradually withdrawing from a warm family scene
- **A slow following move = everyday company** — follow the character's everyday path through the house, using the steadiness of the movement to carry "the everyday rhythm of this home"
- **The bystander's point of view** — the camera is like a quiet member of the family, watching silently from one side. It does not take part and does not judge, it is simply present, with tenderness

---

## 4. Duration control

- **Everyday shots 3-4s** — everyday shots such as eating, chatting and housework keep an unhurried rhythm without dragging
- **Silent shots 4-5s** — the silent company between family members (sitting together saying nothing, each doing their own thing) needs enough duration for the audience to feel that "the silence itself is happiness"
- **特写 on a detail 2-3s** — a 特写 on a warm detail of daily life (a cup of hot tea / a folded piece of clothing) gets enough hold for the audience to "read" the love behind it
- **远景 watching them go 5-6s** — the 远景 of a farewell or a wait needs longer for the emotion of "keeping watch" to ferment fully
- **Dialogue in 近景 3-4s** — family dialogue runs unhurried, with natural silences possible between lines
- **The golden rule: no rushing** — every rhythm in family narrative should carry "the composure of a home". Hurry = anxiety, composure = ease

---

## 5. Shot-merging strategy (removing the AI feel)

- **Run the everyday flow through one shot** — everyday family passages (cooking → carrying the dishes → sitting down → starting to eat) should as far as possible be done in one long take with camera movement, in a single breath; fragmented cutting destroys "the continuity of daily life"
- **Cover a dining-table scene in one shot** — when a scene of the whole family eating can be covered by one shot panning slowly across everyone, do not split it into fragmented cuts of one person at a time
- **Narrative density first** — every shot must advance (推进) the narrative or carry emotional warmth; a purely decorative display of the space should be merged into a shot that has a narrative function
- **The director's-thinking test** — once the storyboard is written, self-check: do the everyday passages have a smooth feeling of daily life? Do the emotional passages hold long enough? Does the fragmented cutting break the steady rhythm of the home?

---

## 6. One-take (merging into a long take)

- **When it applies** — an everyday path through the house, coming home from the door to the dining table, the whole process from cooking to serving. The value of a long take in family narrative is "letting the audience stay in this home for a while"
- **Typical scenes** — the character comes in → changes shoes → walks to the kitchen → sees the meal left on the table; the whole process of cooking (washing → chopping → into the pan → out of the pan); seeing the child to the door → watching them go
- **How to mark it** — state the full path in the camera movement field (for example "一镜到底：the character pushes the door open and comes in → follow past the living room → into the kitchen, sees the meal on the table → close on the character standing still and smiling"), and give the opening and closing framings in the shot description
- **Relaxed duration** — because an everyday family long take keeps renewing its information and needs the rhythm of daily life, it may exceed the 6s single-shot limit, but not 12s
- **The rhythm of life comes first** — the speed of movement in a long take should match the character's living rhythm, neither fast nor slow, carrying "the everyday of this home"

---

## 7. Designing character interaction

- **Family interaction runs on habitual actions** — interaction in a family is not improvised, it is "habitual": always refilling the other's rice, always sitting in the same fixed (固定) place, always calling the other by the same name in the same way
- **Write the unspoken love as action** — in the storyboard table, family feeling is carried first of all by shots of action: an extra piece of food put in the bowl, the good dish pushed towards the other person, the first thing on coming in being a glance to check whether the other is there
- **Physical distance is security** — closeness in a family is "getting near without needing a reason": leaning on a shoulder naturally, holding a hand without thinking, a light pat in passing

---

## 8. Dialogue and the space around it

- **Ordinary talk is the most powerful** — the most moving dialogue in family narrative is ordinary talk: "have you eaten", "get some sleep", "take it slow on the road", "call me when you arrive". No profound lines are needed; everyday care is the deepest love
- **The important things are exactly what cannot be said** — key emotional expression in family narrative should be replaced by silence or action wherever possible. "I love you" cannot be said out loud, but a bowl of soup can be quietly filled for the other person
- **One line per shot** — family dialogue runs slow; leave a natural silence between lines. Do not cram too much dialogue into one shot

---

## 9. Transition design

- **Transition the everyday on the rhythm of time** — to express the repetition of family life, transition naturally on the rhythm of time: "morning → midday → evening", "spring → summer → autumn → winter"
- **Change scene on a detail of daily life** — bridge between different scenes with one empty shot of a detail of daily life (a steaming pot / washing hung out on the balcony / a lit window)
- **Change section on a slow dissolve** — between large sections (from "the everyday" to "the farewell", for instance) use a slow dissolve, carrying the tender passing of time
- **Hard cut by default** — shots within the same scene are joined by hard cuts, keeping the narrative clean and natural
