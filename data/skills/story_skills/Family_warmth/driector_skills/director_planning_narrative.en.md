# Narrative technique · Family Warmth · technique reference

---

## 1. Theme, premise and the emotional core

### Key points for family-warmth narrative

- **The ordinary is the great** — the force of family narrative is not in dramatic large events but in day-after-day company, compromise and unconditional love. The theme and premise should focus on "the seemingly plain days that make up the ground colour of a life"
- **The love that reaches the lips and cannot be said** — the core contradiction of family feeling: the deepest love is delivered in the clumsiest way. The father never says "I love you" but gets up early every morning to cook breakfast; the mother nags but stuffs the suitcase full of food. The premise should circle "the love that is never spoken"
- **The echo across generations** — the most moving moment in family narrative is the "generational repeat": when a child unknowingly makes the same gesture, or says the same words, that a parent did when they were young. That echo gives family narrative "the thickness of time"
- **Suggested directions for the exit feeling** — warmth / homesickness / a sudden sting in the eyes / wanting to call the family. Avoid deliberately manipulative directions such as "tear-jerking" or "wallowing in misery"
- **The sacredness of the everyday** — the family sitting round a meal, watching television and chatting idly, picking a child up from school — in family narrative these everyday scenes are not "connective tissue", they are the core passages with the most narrative value. Their repetition is itself the definition of "happiness"

## 2. Narrative structure and rhythm planning

### Key points for family-warmth narrative

- **The everyday flow is the fundamental skill** — the rhythm of family narrative should sit close to "the rhythm of life": slow, repetitive, regular. Getting up → cooking → going out → coming home → eating → resting; that everyday loop is itself the narrative structure
- **The emotional undercurrent** — on the surface it is everyday trivia, but underneath there should be an emotional undercurrent advancing (推进) slowly (intimacy drifting apart / parents ageing / a child about to leave home). The undercurrent need not be stated; the audience will feel it
- **Generational counterpoint** — the story lines of two generations can be set against each other: the father's choice back then vs the child's choice now; the mother's dream when she was young vs the daughter's pursuit today. The counterpoint holds both inheritance and conflict
- **Crisis and return** — the climax of family narrative is usually not "conflict breaking out" but "it nearly fell apart and in the end it came back together". The threat of separation is what makes the reunion precious
- **Turn the story on an action, not a speech** — reconciliation in a family conflict does not run on apologetic dialogue, it runs on an action: silently bringing over a glass of water, putting the dish the other person likes in front of them, going to the station in the rain to meet them. An action has more of the texture of family narrative than a thousand words
- **The "last meal" model** — the tear point of family narrative is usually a "last time" of something everyday: the last home-cooked meal before a wedding, the last dinner in the old house before moving, parents seeing a child off from home. "The last time" gives the everyday an unbearable weight

## 3. Per-scene emotional design

### Key points for family-warmth narrative

- **State the emotional target in the felt language of life** — do not say "warm", say "one mouthful of hot soup and the warmth goes from the stomach to the heart". Do not say "missing someone", say "she finds the old photograph, picks it up and puts it down again". Family emotion is always concrete and touchable
- **Typical emotional passages and their design** —

| Passage type | Emotional direction | Narrative technique | Music suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Everyday reunion | Warm + at ease | Eating / chatting / housework, steady rhythm, rich detail | An extremely light bed, or pure sounds of life |
| Clumsy caring | Moved + aching | Care from someone who cannot express it: slipping money over, packing an umbrella, calling in secret | No scoring, let the action speak for itself |
| Generational conflict | Hurt + baffled | Talking past each other, the silent treatment, a turned back and a slammed door | Sudden quiet, only breathing and the sound of the door |
| Parting / seeing off | Reluctance + restraint | Station / doorway / airport, saying it is fine while the hand does not let go | A single minimal instrument, or pure ambience |
| Reconciliation / return | Release + gratitude | An embrace / a meal / one line: "you're back then" | Warm instrumental easing slowly in |
| Inheritance / echo | The tenderness of time | The child does what the parent did, the generational feeling closing the loop | A warm variation on the theme melody |

- **"Feeling carried by objects" is the core narrative tool** — the transmission of feeling in family narrative depends heavily on concrete objects:
  - **Food**: a bowl of soup / a dish / a lunchbox = the most direct expression of love
  - **Old things**: an old photograph / old clothes / a drawing from childhood = the warmth of time
  - **Keys / the door**: giving a key = this is your home; leaving the door open = I will always be waiting for you
- **Space is belonging** — in family narrative the space of "home" is itself the most important character. The kitchen = where love is produced; the living room = where the family intersects; the child's room = independence and worry; the old house = the container of memory
- **The tenderness of the "bystander" point of view** — the advanced technique of family narrative is "the bystander sees what the people involved do not": the child does not see the mother wiping away tears behind him, the father does not see the child watching him through the crack of the door

## 4. Sound and music direction

### Key points for family-warmth narrative

- **A home has its own soundscape** — the scrape of a spatula in a wok, the gurgle of a kettle coming to the boil, slippers slapping on the floor, the blurred sound of the television — these sounds make up "the sound of home"; mark 1-2 signature family sound effects in every scene
- **The sounds of life are the score** — the most powerful "score" in family narrative is often the sounds of life themselves: the rhythm of chopping vegetables, water running over the washing-up, the clatter of a sewing machine. These sounds do not need scoring over them; they are the melody of the home
- **The silence of family love** — the deepest feeling between family members is often in silence: father and son sitting together saying nothing, mother and daughter each doing their own thing in the same room. Strip the scoring from the key family passages and leave only the ambience of daily life
- **Extreme restraint in the scoring** — family-warmth scoring coverage should not exceed 40%. Over-scoring turns warmth into manipulation; restrained scoring lets the audience be moved on their own
- **Music-emotion progression model** —

| Emotional stage | Music strategy | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Steady everyday | Pure sounds of life, or an extremely light bed | Very low |
| Undercurrent stirring | A single instrument at low volume | Low |
| Conflict breaking out | Sudden total silence | None |
| Reconciliation, warming | Warm instrumental easing slowly in | Medium |
| Generational echo | The theme melody presented in full | Medium-high |

- **The sound of "coming home"** — the most ritual sound effect in family narrative is "the sound of coming home": the key in the door, a shout of "I'm back" on the way in, the sound of slippers being set down. Every time these sounds appear they repeat the reassurance that "home is still here"

## 5. Composition and shot-size narrative

### Key points for family-warmth narrative

- **The narrative function of the three core compositions** —
  - **Several people in one frame** — the family together in one picture (eating / on the sofa / posing for a photograph), carrying the belonging of "we are one family"
  - **Foreground-background layering** — one person in front doing something, one behind watching silently, carrying "the care you cannot see"
  - **Doorframe / window-frame composition** — family seen through a doorframe = a protected space / a space about to be left / a picture inside a memory
- **The dining table is the core narrative space** — the dining table appears more often than anything else in family narrative. Subtle changes in the table composition map the family relations: every seat full = reunion; one seat empty = loss; only two people = depending on each other alone
- **Distance is the relationship** — physical distance between family members maps emotional distance: squeezed together = intimacy / ease; sitting apart = a cold war / estrangement; one standing while one sits = an imbalance of power or of care
- **The keeping watch of 远景** — the core function of 远景 (long shot) in family narrative is "keeping watch": the figure standing at the door watching them go, the silhouette at the window waiting for a return, the back walking further and further away
- **The family function of 大特写** — 大特写 (extreme close-up) catches the details of feeling in a family that "cannot be said": a mother's trembling lips, a father's hand clenching and opening again, a child's eyes quietly gone red
- **The layers of time in a space** — when the same family space (the living room / the kitchen / the child's room) recurs across different passages, subtle changes of detail carry the passage of time: more or fewer photographs on the wall, the furniture worn, the toys of before turned into textbooks

## 6. Camera movement and rhythm

### Key points for family-warmth narrative

- **Mostly steady, slow moves** — the camera movement of family narrative should convey "steadiness / ease". Slow, level, unhurried. 70% or more of the shots are 静止 (static) or move very slowly
- **缓推 (slow push in) = noticing / drawing near** — the psychological cue of "the audience has noticed a warm detail"; good for discovering the good deed a character did in secret, for noticing the meal left on the table, for seeing the shoes set out at the door
- **Slow pull back = leaving / watching them go** — the psychological cue of "the audience or the character stepping back"; good for seeing someone off, for leaving home, for withdrawing from a family scene
- **The bystander's gaze** — a large share of shots in family narrative should feel like "a bystander watching quietly". The camera does not take part and does not intrude, it simply watches this family in silence — like a gentle witness
- **Everyday following** — follow the character's everyday path through the house (from the kitchen to the dining room carrying dishes, from the door to the living room putting things down), using a slow following move to carry the rhythm of daily life
- **Camera movement is the temperature of the home** — 静止 = steadiness / company; 缓推 = drawing near / attention; slow pull back = parting / watching them go; a slow following move = the everyday / company; 摇镜 (pan) sweeping across the family = taking stock of happiness
