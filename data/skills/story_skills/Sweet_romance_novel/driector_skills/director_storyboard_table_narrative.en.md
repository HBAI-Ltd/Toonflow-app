# Storyboard-table narrative technique · Sweet Romance · technique reference

---

## 1. What the storyboard table is for

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into shot language. The fields of the form are set by the director as the project requires (shot number, shot size, camera movement, duration, characters, events, dialogue, lighting, emotion, transitions and so on); what follows is only a technique reference for the sweet romance narrative genre.

---

## 2. Choosing the shot size

- **Shot-size escalation in a sweet-romance scene** — within one scene the shot size should escalate as the emotion warms: half body → 近景 (medium close-up) → 特写 (close-up) → 大特写 (extreme close-up). Do not shove a 特写 in at the start; leave room for the emotion to rise
- **远景 is not a connector** — a 远景 (long shot) shot has narrative value in itself (solitude, spatial pressure, the mood of the season). Give 远景 enough duration (4-6s) and do not rush to cut away
- **大特写 needs a reason** — 大特写 (eyes / lips / hands) is an emotional warhead; 2-3 times per episode is enough. Overuse tires the audience out
- **Keep the establishing shot lean** — establish (the establishing shot) in 1-2 shots at most; do not split it into three or more fragments. The typical approach: 1 大远景 (extreme long shot) / 远景 to establish + 1 全景 (wide shot) to introduce the subject, or a single 远景 with a 缓推 (slow push in) that establishes and introduces at once. Avoid the redundant three-part "shoot the environment → then a detail → then the character arriving"

---

## 3. Camera-movement rhythm

- **静止 by default** — 60% or more of the shots should be on a 静止 (static) camera, letting the detail in the picture and the emotion speak for themselves
- **缓推 = emotional escalation** — the psychological cue that "the audience is drawing closer to the character"; it suits a flutter of the heart, a discovery, a stolen look
- **Slow pull back = emotional detachment** — the psychological cue that "the audience is backing away"; it suits parting, solitude, revealing the whole picture
- **Camera movement is emotion** — camera movement is not a technical choice, it is an emotional choice. 静止 = composure / suppression; 缓推 = drawing closer / a flutter; slow pull back = detachment / solitude; slow pan = display / solemnity
- **The sweet-romance "heartbeat move"** — in a warming, ambiguous passage use a tiny 缓推 together with shot-size escalation (half body → 近景 → 特写) to simulate the physical sensation of attention narrowing as the heart races

---

## 4. Duration control

- **特写 / expression shots** — 2-3s, focused on the change of micro-expression
- **Dialogue in 近景** — 3-4s, the line delivered steadily
- **Full-body entrance** — 3-5s, showing the whole figure
- **远景 / empty shots** — 4-6s, building atmosphere
- **No single shot over 6s** — past 6s the audience's attention decays and camera movement or a moving element is needed to hold it
- **The golden 6-second rule** — if a 无台词 shot runs past 6s in total with no new information (dialogue / action / a change of subject), the audience's attention breaks. Watch establishing and transitional shots especially; better to merge and compress than to let it drag

---

## 5. Shot-merging strategy (removing the AI feel)

- **Do not split into two shots what one shot can state** — if one shot with camera movement (for example a 缓推 from 远景 to 全景) can establish and introduce the subject at the same time, do not split it into two shots of "empty shot to establish → cut to the subject entering frame"
- **Merge consecutive information of the same kind** — consecutive shots describing different parts of the same space (courtyard gate → vines → the fire-blackened side room) should be merged into one shot, with the shot description covering the several layers of spatial information
- **Narrative density first** — every shot must advance the narrative or the emotion; a purely decorative shot (there only to display a detail of the environment) should be merged into a shot that has a narrative function
- **The director's-thinking test** — once the storyboard is written, self-check: if a real director would shoot 2-3 adjacent shots as a single shot, you have cut it too fine and should merge

---

## 6. One-take (merging into a long take)

- **When it applies** — when adjacent shots hold a continuous change of action, a light change of scene (movement within the same scene) or a gradual change of shooting angle, prefer a one-take over fragmented cutting; the picture and the content flow better
- **Typical scenes** — a character walking across a space, following an action from point A to point B, 环绕 (orbit) around a character to show the environment, establishing then a 缓推 in to a 特写 of the subject
- **How to mark it** — state the full path in the camera movement field (for example "一镜到底：缓推 from 远景 → track into the courtyard → close on 全景"), and give the opening and closing framings in the shot description
- **Relaxed duration** — because the information keeps renewing, it may exceed the 6s single-shot limit, but not 12s
- **Generation risk** — a one-take demands high continuity from image generation and is harder to land. Use it only where the gain in narrative flow clearly outweighs fragmented cutting; no more than 2-3 places in the whole film

---

## 7. Designing character interaction

- **No more than two actions in one shot** — "lowering the head to pick a flower + a smile" is fine; "lowering the head to pick a flower + a smile + turning round + raising a hand" will break
- **Sweet-romance interaction runs on suggestion** — fingers that just miss touching, a sleeve brushing past, a gaze that follows and then looks away. Do not write large two-person interactions such as "an embrace" or "a kiss" into the storyboard table; break them into suggestive partial shots

---

## 8. Dialogue and the space around it

- **Give the shots with little dialogue a long duration** — an emotional shot with 无台词 usually needs more time than one with dialogue. Three seconds of silence carries more tension than a line
- **One line, one shot** — avoid packing several lines into a single shot; cut to a new shot when the speaker changes
- **Put narration shots in 远景 or on an empty shot** — inner monologue over a 近景 easily looks fake because the lips do not move; over a 远景 or an empty scene shot it is more natural

---

## 9. Transition design

- **Hard cut by default** — shots within the same scene are joined by hard cuts, clean and decisive
- **Bridge scene changes with an empty shot** — insert 1 empty scene shot (2-3s) between different scenes as an emotional buffer
- **Passage changes may use a dissolve or a fade** — use a soft transition for the emotional jump between large passages, so the audience is not thrown out of the story
