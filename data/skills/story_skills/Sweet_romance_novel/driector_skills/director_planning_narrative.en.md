# Narrative technique · Sweet Romance · technique reference

---

## 1. Theme, premise and the emotional core

### Key points for sweet romance narrative

- **Restraint before statement** — emotion is not carried by laying it out in dialogue, it is carried by what is left unsaid and by tiny reactions. The theme and premise should lean towards restraint and understatement, and avoid open sentimentality
- **Sweetness held back** — "almost touching" works better than "stuck together". The emotional main line should be built as a push-pull rhythm of "about to speak and stopping"; the sweetness comes from what the audience fills in for themselves
- **Small things carry the weight** — do not chase emotional impact through big set pieces; move people with detail: one look, one sentence swallowed, a hem of clothing blown loose by the wind
- **Suggested directions for the exit feeling** — aching for them / unresolved longing / a fluttering heart / healing. Avoid directions that do not match the sweet-romance temperament, such as "gratification" or "adrenaline"
- **Warmth inside coldness, closeness inside distance** — sweet romance is not the same as cloying. The overall key may be cool and distant, but warmth is released at the key beats; the contrast is the greatest sweetness

## 2. Narrative structure and rhythm planning

### Key points for sweet romance narrative

- **Slowness is the basic skill** — sweet romance carries a high density of emotional information (looks, micro-expressions, physical distance) and the audience needs time to "feel" it. The overall rhythm leans slow, but slow is not sluggish — every passage adds emotional increment
- **Keep the emotional curve on a gentle slope** — avoid "flat, flat, flat → sudden explosion". Use progressive emotional escalation, each passage one degree more concentrated than the one before
- **Turn the story on action, not dialogue** — key turning points should be handled first by means of action (a look that changes, a change in physical distance, silence, a prop handed over) rather than by relying on dialogue to explain
- **Bridge passages with an emotional buffer** — the join between passages needs an emotional buffer, not a hard cut. Use an empty environment shot, a moment alone or a fragment of everyday life as breathing space
- **The "speed" of a climax passage is not fast cutting** — it is high emotional density. Build the racing-heart feeling with tighter shot-size changes (full body → 近景 (medium close-up) → 特写 (close-up) → 大特写 (extreme close-up)) rather than by shortening how long you hold
- **The push-pull rhythm model** — the core engine of sweet romance is "push and pull": closer → pulling back → closer again → misunderstanding → separation → reunion. Every round of push-pull should go deeper, hurt more and taste sweeter than the last

## 3. Per-scene emotional design

### Key points for sweet romance narrative

- **State the emotional target in concrete words** — do not say "happy", say "the corner of her mouth she cannot keep down after a secret flutter". A concrete description of the emotion guides the choice of shot size and the detail of the performance far better
- **Typical emotional passages and their design** —

| Passage type | Emotional direction | Narrative technique | Music suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|
| First meeting / entrance | Struck + curious | "Discover" the other person from an onlooker's point of view, far before near | Leave it bare, use only ambience to build a held breath |
| Everyday secret love | Undercurrent + restraint | Stolen glances, words swallowed, distance kept on purpose | Soft instrumental, low volume, under the scene |
| Misunderstanding / separation | Heartache + endurance | No explanation, a turned back, tears shed alone | A grieving solo, or pure ambience |
| Confession / reconciliation | Release + flutter | Speaking after silence, the eyes ahead of the words | From quiet into warm instrumental fading in |
| Warming up / ambiguity | Tension + sweetness | Physical distance closing, a light touch, breath audible | A light rhythmic entry hinting at a heartbeat |
| High sweetness / wedding | Happiness + solemnity | Ceremony, a grave meeting of eyes, a promise | Full instrumental, solemn but tender |

- **"Distance" is the core narrative tool** — map the progress of the relationship onto the physical distance between the characters:
  - **Early**: 远景 (long shot) / half body, large physical distance, polite words
  - **Middle**: 近景, distance closed but with something in between (an object / a crowd / hesitation)
  - **Late**: 特写 / 大特写, zero distance, every psychological defence laid down
- **Spatial elements are emotional metaphors** — use elements of the scene to carry emotion and lean less on dialogue. For example: a blurred figure behind a curtain = estrangement; pushing a door open onto a courtyard in full bloom = release; sitting alone in the rain = solitude
- **Write shot intent as "why", not "how to shoot"** — "the 特写 is there so the audience sees the hesitation in her eyes" beats "shoot her face in 特写". Once the intent is clear, the storyboard picks the right shot size and angle by itself

## 4. Sound and music direction

### Key points for sweet romance narrative

- **Silence is stronger than score** — at key emotional instants (a meeting of eyes, a tear falling, a turn and exit) consider first dropping the score and leaving only ambience. The "sweet" in sweet romance is usually what the audience fills in for themselves after the silence
- **Score follows the passage** — do not score scene by scene; divide by passage and set one musical emotional key per passage. Within a passage, bridge scene changes with a change of ambience rather than switching cues often
- **Do not score wall to wall** — keep total score coverage under 60% of the film. The silence of the bare passages and the scored passages together create breathing
- **Ambience is half the atmosphere** — mark 1-2 core ambient sounds for every scene to help the later sound design. The richer the layers of ambience, the more immersive the scene
- **Music-emotion escalation model** —

| Emotional stage | Music strategy | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Steady / everyday | Soft instrumental under the scene | Low |
| Undercurrent / building | A single instrument solo, very low volume | Medium-low |
| Emotional release | Instrumental filling out, or sudden silence | Medium-high |
| Turn of fate | Strong instrumental or total silence | Extreme |
| Warming again / healing | Warm instrumental easing in | Medium |

- **The "heartbeat" of sweet romance** — in a warming, ambiguous passage a light percussive pulse (hand drum, wood block, plucked strings) can suggest a quickening heartbeat; it is more sophisticated than reaching straight for a sweet melody

## 5. Composition and shot-size narrative

### Key points for sweet romance narrative

- **The narrative function of the three core compositions** —
  - **Large negative space** — loneliness / mood / poetic space, carrying the character's psychological isolation or an emotional blank
  - **Framed composition** — a figure behind gauze, a doorframe, a window lattice or a screen, creating the secret-love point of view of "watching in secret" and a sense of separation
  - **Rule of thirds** — dialogue / everyday life / two-person interaction, stable and balanced, suited to everyday sweetness
- **Restricted use of centred composition** — save centred composition for formal entrances and ceremonial scenes (a wedding, a formal confession). Do not use it for everyday moments, or the impact of ceremony is lost
- **Spatial depth is narrative** — foreground occlusion (a curtain / a flowering branch / mist) + the subject in 中景 (medium shot) + the environment in 远景 (long shot); the more layers, the stronger the estrangement, the fewer layers, the greater the intimacy
- **Vertical and horizontal framing** — single-character 特写 and entrances lean vertical (stressing solitude and the quality of the figure); two-person shots and scenes lean horizontal (stressing the relationship and the space they share)
- **Sweet-romance shot-size escalation** — within one scene the shot size should escalate as the emotion warms: half body → 近景 → 特写 → 大特写. Do not shove a 特写 in at the start; leave room for the emotion to rise
- **大特写 needs a reason** — 大特写 (eyes / lips / hands) is an emotional warhead; 2-3 times per episode is enough. Overuse tires the audience out
- **远景 is not a connector** — a 远景 shot has narrative value in itself (solitude, spatial pressure, the mood of the season). Give 远景 enough duration (4-6s) and do not rush to cut away

## 6. Camera movement and rhythm

### Key points for sweet romance narrative

- **Let stillness govern movement** — 60% or more of the shots should be on a 静止 (static) camera, letting the detail in the picture and the emotion speak for themselves
- **缓推 (slow push in) = drawing closer / a flutter** — the psychological cue that "the audience is drawing closer to the character"; it suits a flutter of the heart, a discovery, a stolen look
- **Slow pull back = detachment / solitude** — the psychological cue that "the audience is backing away"; it suits parting, solitude, revealing the whole picture
- **Fast, fragmented cutting does not belong here** — fast cutting is incompatible with the temperament of sweet romance. Even in a climax passage, build rhythm through shot-size escalation rather than fast cutting
- **摇镜 (pan) and tracking** — a slow pan suits showing the whole of a scene or following a character walking; tracking suits ceremonial or walking scenes. Both should be kept restrained in speed
- **Camera movement is emotion** — camera movement is not a technical choice, it is an emotional choice. 静止 = composure / suppression; 缓推 = drawing closer / a flutter; slow pull back = detachment / solitude; slow pan = display / solemnity
- **The sweet-romance "heartbeat move"** — in a warming, ambiguous passage use a tiny 缓推 together with shot-size escalation (half body → 近景 → 特写) to simulate the physical sensation of attention narrowing as the heart races
