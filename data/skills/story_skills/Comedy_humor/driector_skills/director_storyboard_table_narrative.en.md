# Storyboard-table narrative technique · Comedy & Humour · technique reference

---

## 1. What the storyboard table is for

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into shot language. The fields of the form are set by the director as the project requires (shot number, shot size, camera movement, duration, characters, events, dialogue, lighting, emotion, transitions and so on); what follows is only a technique reference for the comedy & humour narrative genre.

---

## 2. Choosing the shot size

- **The reaction shot is comedy's trump card** — after a laugh lands you must cut straight to a "reaction shot" (the bystander's face / the victim petrified), caught in 近景 (medium close-up). A laugh with no reaction shot is a dud
- **全景 exposes information** — the core function of 全景 (wide shot) in comedy is "letting the audience see the absurdity the character cannot see": the person behind him, the anomaly in the background, the disaster about to happen. 全景 = giving the audience the god's-eye view
- **中景 (medium shot) carries the dialogue** — it is the workhorse shot size for character interaction and collision, holding enough information for the audience to read body language and expression
- **特写 freezes the awkwardness** — in comedy, 特写 (close-up) magnifies the awkward detail: the misspelled word, the misplaced object, the frozen smile. It holds only briefly (1-2s) but hits hard enough
- **The establishing shot needs a seed of comedy** — a comic establishing shot can bury one "off" detail inside an apparently normal scene (someone doing something strange in the background / a slogan with a typo) as setup for the laugh to come

---

## 3. Camera-movement rhythm

- **Mostly a 固定 camera** — 60% or more of the shots are 固定 (locked off); the cool bystander's point of view makes the absurdity stand out
- **Fast push in = magnified reaction** — after the laugh, push fast into the reactor's face to magnify the shock / petrification / disbelief. It is the most frequently used camera movement in comic storyboards
- **甩镜 (whip pan) = revealing the absurd** — whip fast from A to B, letting the speed itself carry the surprise of "what?!"
- **Handheld tracking for loss of control** — when characters fall into chaos (a chase, a flight, all hands flailing) use handheld tracking; the shake carries "everything is out of control"
- **缓推 (slow push in) = discovering a detail** — push slowly into an absurd detail to give the audience the discovery laugh of "wait, let me see that properly… ha!"
- **The camera does not upstage the performance** — the principle of comic camera movement is "do not steal the character's scene". The simpler the movement the better; fancy camera work pulls attention away from the laugh

---

## 4. Duration control

- **Give the setup enough time** — setup shots run 3-4s so the audience can fully build the expectation. If the setup is not full enough, the payoff does not land
- **Hit the payoff on the exact beat** — the shot that drops the payoff runs 1.5-2s, crisp and clean. The force of a laugh is in its suddenness; drag it out and it stops being funny
- **Reaction shot 2-3s** — give the bystander's or the victim's reaction enough "freeze" time for the audience to digest the laugh
- **Leave air after the laugh** — leave 1-2s of "blank" after the laugh (the character silent / the air frozen) to give the audience time to laugh. If you advance the narrative at once, the laughter is cut off
- **The golden 6-second rule** — comic rhythm requires a single shot to stay under 4s as a rule (faster than other genres); only setup and true-feeling passages may reach 5-6s
- **Duration progression in the rule of three** — when the same laugh repeats three times: first pass at normal duration → second pass slightly shorter (the audience already knows the expectation) → third pass either shortest or longest (breaking the pattern)

---

## 5. Storyboarding the laugh

- **The setup→payoff shot structure** — setup shot (中景/近景, the character doing something in earnest) → payoff shot (全景 revealing the absurdity / 特写 magnifying the error / 近景 on the petrified reaction). The two shots together make one laugh unit
- **A visual laugh needs no dialogue** — a purely visual laugh (the character stepping on something, an absurd event in the background, a prop in the wrong place) is presented in 全景 or in a 中景 with camera movement, without relying on dialogue
- **The information-gap laugh** — information the audience has and the character does not creates the laugh of anticipation. On the storyboard: 全景 gives the audience the information → cut back to the character in 中景/近景 (blissfully unaware) → the disaster happens
- **The chain-reaction laugh** — one small mistake sets off a domino collapse. On the storyboard, string each "falling" link together with fast hard cuts (2s per shot), the rhythm getting faster and faster
- **Do not split a comic scene that can be shot in one** — a character sliding from normal into total chaos across one long take, all of it in a single shot, carries more of the "no way back" comedy than a fragmented cut

---

## 6. Blocking multi-character comedy

- **The group reaction chain** — after the laugh lands, cut to each bystander's reaction in turn: A stunned → B stifling a laugh → C pretending not to have seen → D more embarrassed than the victim. The reaction chain is itself the continuation of the laugh
- **Shoot character collision in shot-reverse-shot** — dialogue between two sharply contrasting personalities is cut in shot-reverse-shot with the rhythm speeding up (1.5-2s per line), so the collision is externalised through cutting speed
- **The bystander is the best amplifier of a laugh** — always keep one "bystander slot" in the storyboard: a passer-by, an unrelated character, a pet — their reaction helps the audience "confirm" that this really is absurd
- **Use 全景 for collective loss of control** — when several people fall into chaos at once, present it in one 全景 so the audience sees all the absurdity simultaneously. The explosion of information in 全景 is itself the comedy

---

## 7. Dialogue and rhythm

- **The beat of the line decides the laugh** — the laugh in comic dialogue is in the beat: the setup delivered at normal speed → a micro-pause before the payoff (0.5s) → the payoff landing crisply in one line. The pause is the fuse of the laugh
- **Snark must be fast** — a snarky comeback must come in within 0.5s of the previous line ending; only a fast counterpunch feels good. In the storyboard, the snark shot gets no extra setup — hard cut straight to 近景
- **The comedy of silence** — after something absurd happens, the characters going collectively silent for 2-3s is funnier than any line. The micro-expressions during that silence (pretending nothing happened / petrified / slowly turning the head) are the body of the laugh
- **The power of repetition** — the same line or the same action repeated three times, with a change on the third (said wrong / done wrong / the character laughing at themselves). On the storyboard, use the same shot size and the same composition for all three passes and break it on the third
- **The snark function of narration** — in comedy, narration can act as the "sharp-tongued commentator", describing the absurd picture in a calm voice. Narration works best over 远景 (long shot) or 全景

---

## 8. Transition design

- **The absurd-contrast cut** — hard cut straight from one extreme to the other (the character says "absolutely not" → the next shot has him doing exactly that); the hard cut is itself the laugh
- **The mock-formal transition** — use a formal or solemn transition device (fade in and out, a title card reading "three hours later") to move into absurd content; the contrast produces the comedy
- **Fast hard cuts within a scene** — comic cutting inside a scene runs fast, mostly hard cuts, no dawdling
- **Contrast montage** — cut between the "beautiful plan" the character describes and the "disaster site" of it actually being done; the contrast of ideal vs reality builds sustained comedy
- **Breaking the fourth wall** — the moment the character suddenly looks into the lens or speaks to the audience, shoot it in frontal 近景 with the camera suddenly 静止 (static), creating the complicity of "you saw that too, right?"
