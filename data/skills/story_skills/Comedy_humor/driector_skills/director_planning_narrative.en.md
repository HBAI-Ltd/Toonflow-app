# Narrative technique · Comedy & Humour · technique reference

---

## 1. Theme, premise and the comic core

### Key points for comedy & humour narrative

- **Broken expectation is the engine of the laugh** — the mechanism underneath every laugh is "broken expectation": the audience thinks the character will go left, he goes right; thinks he will cry, he burps. The director's job is to build the expectation first, then break it precisely
- **Character collision produces comedy** — the highest comedy does not run on gags, it runs on the chemical reaction between personalities. Put a dead-serious person next to a person with no dignity and the dialogue is funny by itself. The bigger the contrast in personality, the harder the collision
- **The absurd must have internal logic** — when an absurd thing happens, the character must "take it seriously". The more serious the character, the more absurd it feels to the audience. If the character finds it absurd too, the laugh is gone
- **Suggested directions for the exit feeling** — belly laugh / knowing smile / laughing through tears / release from the absurd. Avoid directions that do not match the comic temperament, such as "fear", "heaviness" or "desolation"
- **Laughing through tears is the highest achievement** — pure gags are forgotten the moment the laugh ends, but when the character's real fragility or kindness suddenly shows through the absurdity, the audience is still laughing when their eyes sting — that emotional reversal is comedy's most powerful weapon

## 2. Narrative structure and comic timing

### Key points for comedy & humour narrative

- **The setup→payoff base unit** — every laugh is made of two parts, the "setup" and the "payoff". The setup builds the expectation (the straighter the better), the payoff breaks it (the more unexpected the better). Neither may be missing
- **The rule of three** — the classic comic rhythm: the first pass establishes the pattern → the second pass reinforces the pattern → the third pass breaks the pattern. On the third pass the audience expects the same thing and does not get it — that is the laugh
- **Escalating absurdity** — the degree of absurdity should escalate step by step: small absurdity → medium absurdity → large absurdity. Opening at full absurdity makes the audience unable to accept it; only by raising the absurdity gradually do you keep the comedy alive
- **No more than 60 seconds between laughs** — in comic narrative the gap between two laughs must not exceed 60 seconds of narrative time. With no comic output for more than 60 seconds the audience's "comic anticipation" goes cold
- **A serious passage is the best setup** — a serious or moving passage laid into a comedy does not interrupt the comedy, it is the longest possible setup for the next laugh. The more moving it is, the bigger the contrast of the laugh that follows
- **Driven by a chain of misunderstandings** — the classic engine of comic narrative is "misunderstanding": A misreads B → B misreads A's misreading → C does not know A and B are both misreading → the misunderstanding snowballs → the truth finally comes out and the whole absurdity resolves into laughter

## 3. Per-scene comic design

### Key points for comedy & humour narrative

- **Describe the comedy through contrast** — do not say "funny", say "with a face of solemn dignity he performs CPR on a goldfish". A concrete description of the contrast is itself what makes people laugh
- **Typical comic passages and their design** —

| Passage type | Emotional direction | Narrative technique | Music suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal opening | Calm + everyday | Everything normal, but the character has a small quirk or fixation | Normal everyday scoring (storing up for the contrast) |
| Small loss of control | Accident + funny | One small accident sets off a chain reaction | Scoring stays normal (the contrast strengthens the comedy) |
| Misunderstanding escalates | Awkward + absurd | The more he explains the worse it gets, the more he patches it the harder it collapses | A light rhythm speeds up to hint at the loss of control |
| Big loss of control | Total chaos | Every character falls into their own absurdity at once | Cheerful scoring at full blast, or sudden total silence |
| True feeling surfaces | Laughing through tears | Real emotion suddenly shows through the absurdity | Scoring cuts out, only ambience left |
| Return / one last payoff | Release + knowing | Once everything has settled, one more small laugh | A relaxed closing melody |

- **Escalating awkwardness is the core comic narrative** — the escalation of awkwardness:
  - **Level one**: the character is awkward on their own (says the wrong thing, falls over)
  - **Level two**: bystanders see the character's awkwardness too (social death)
  - **Level three**: the character tries to patch it and makes it worse (digging deeper)
  - **Level four**: total loss of control, everyone is awkward (collective social death)
- **Space is comedy's accomplice** — use spatial elements to build comedy: too many people crammed into too small a space, a ridiculous event in a place that should be solemn, completely mismatched people showing up in a completely mismatched location
- **Write the shot intent as "where the comedy comes from", not "what is being shot"** — "the 全景 (wide shot) is there so the audience can see the ten oblivious people standing behind him" beats "shoot the location in 全景". Once the source of the comedy is clear, the storyboard falls into place by itself

## 4. Sound and music direction

### Key points for comedy & humour narrative

- **Contrasting scoring is the sharpest comic tool** — an absurd picture scored dead straight (classical music, a solemn march) is funnier than one scored cheerfully. The contrast between sound and picture is itself a "broken expectation"
- **Sudden silence = the biggest laugh** — busy scoring stops dead while the character on screen does something utterly absurd — absurdity in silence hits harder than absurdity with music under it
- **Avoid "comedy scoring"** — do not use scoring that "already sounds funny" (clown horns, comedy percussion sound effects). Let the picture generate the comedy while the scoring stays neutral or even serious; the comedy comes out of the contrast
- **The comic function of ambience** — a specific ambient sound can become part of the laugh: a phone ringing in the distance during a solemn speech, a stomach growling in a silent exam hall
- **Music-emotion model** —

| Emotional stage | Music strategy | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Normal setup | Straight scoring (a contrast against the absurdity to come) | Medium |
| Small loss of control | Scoring unchanged (pretending everything is normal) | Medium |
| Big loss of control | Scoring accelerates or stops dead | Extreme |
| Release of the laugh | A brief silence, then back in | Low→medium |
| True feeling surfaces | Scoring cuts out, pure ambience | None |
| Return to the everyday | A relaxed close | Medium-low |

- **"Metronome" comedy** — a repeating laugh (the same mistake happening again and again) can be reinforced with a background sound on a fixed (固定) tempo that builds the "here we go again" expectation; breaking the rhythm on the third pass maximises the laugh

## 5. Composition and shot-size narrative

### Key points for comedy & humour narrative

- **The narrative function of the three core compositions** —
  - **Reaction composition** — put the source of the laugh in the foreground or off screen and the reactor at the centre of frame. Very often the bystander's face is funnier than the laugh itself
  - **Exposing composition in 全景** — 全景 reveals information the character does not have (there is someone behind him / his trousers have split / something absurd is in the background), letting the audience see one layer more than the character
  - **Mismatched juxtaposition** — two unmatched elements in one frame: a man in a suit and a chicken, a solemn ceremony and a chaotic background; the mismatched juxtaposition is itself the comedy
- **The "spotlight" effect of centred composition** — in comedy, centred composition creates the social death of "the whole world is watching you humiliate yourself"; save it for the character's most awkward moment
- **The information gap in the frame** — the core of comic composition is "the audience sees what the character does not": on the left of frame the character is giving an earnest speech, on the right someone is holding up a banner with a typo in it
- **Shot size is comic rhythm** —
  - **远景 (long shot) / 全景** — expose the absurdity of the whole situation, reveal information the character does not have
  - **中景 (medium shot)** — standard narrative, dialogue and interaction, character collision
  - **近景 (medium close-up)** — the core shot size for a reaction shot, catching the "huh?" and "what?" faces
  - **特写 (close-up)** — magnify the awkward detail: the twitching corner of a mouth, a frozen smile, a forehead breaking into a cold sweat
- **The "freeze-frame laugh" of 大特写** — in comedy 大特写 (extreme close-up) does the job of "freezing and magnifying": a hand in the wrong place, a name badge spelled wrong, a face that does not match anything happening. Holding for 1-2s is enough

## 6. Camera movement and rhythm

### Key points for comedy & humour narrative

- **Watching coolly from the outside is comedy's best point of view** — 60% or more of the shots should be on a 固定 (locked off) camera, recording the absurdity in the posture of a "calm bystander". The cooler the camera, the sharper the contrast of the absurdity in frame
- **Fast push in = magnified reaction** — a character hears or sees something absurd and the camera pushes fast into their face, magnifying the "petrified / stunned / cannot believe it" reaction. This is the highest-frequency camera movement in comedy
- **甩镜 (whip pan) = revealing information** — whipping fast from character A to character B or to something absurd; the speed itself carries the comedy of "wait! what is that?!"
- **Steady vs chaotic** — use a steady camera for setup passages (everything is normal); in loss-of-control passages the camera may go slightly unsteady or even handheld-tracking (going along with the chaos). The switch from steady to unsteady itself marks "the funny part is starting"
- **Camera movement is rhythm** — 固定 = calm observation / setup; fast push in = magnified reaction; 甩镜 = revealing information; handheld tracking = following the chaos; 缓推 (slow push in) = discovering the absurd detail
- **The comic function of slow motion** — in comedy, overcranking is for "the instant the disaster happens": the object flying off, the person slipping, the cake hitting the face — slow motion gives the audience enough time to "foresee the disaster" without being able to stop it, and the laugh builds up inside that foresight
- **Do not move the camera often** — comedy needs no fancy camera work. The simpler and plainer the shot, the more the character's absurdity stands out. Too much camera movement pulls attention away from the laugh
