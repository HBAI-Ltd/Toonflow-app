# Narrative technique · Coming of Age · technique reference

---

## 1. Theme, premise and the emotional core

### Key points for coming-of-age narrative

- **The preciousness of the first time** — the core appeal of coming-of-age narrative is the impact of the "first time": the first crush, the first failure, the first goodbye, the first time standing up for someone else. The theme and premise should circle the vividness and panic of "feeling something for the first time in a life"
- **Clumsiness is truth** — the way youth expresses itself is clumsy by nature. The wrong words, the wrong moves, the courage to chase back what you have already lost — a perfect youth does not move anyone, a flailing one does
- **Irreversible time is the base emotion** — every sweetness and every regret in coming-of-age narrative shares one ground colour: "there is no going back". That sense of irreversibility gives every tiny moment its weight
- **Suggested directions for the exit feeling** — nostalgia / release / a smile with tears in it / wanting to go back to that day. Avoid utilitarian directions such as "a rush of satisfaction" or "the underdog's revenge"
- **The pain of growing up** — coming-of-age narrative cannot be all sweetness. Real growth comes out of pain: the crack in a friendship, the collapse of an ideal, the first realisation that "the world is not what I thought it was". Sweetness is the surface; pain is the engine of growth

## 2. Narrative structure and rhythm planning

### Key points for coming-of-age narrative

- **Fragmented narrative is the natural form** — the memory of youth is fragmented to begin with: one sentence between classes, the sunset on the way home from school, the rain that suddenly fell one day. The narrative structure may accept a more fragmented organisation of passages, strung together by an emotional line rather than an event line
- **The emotional rollercoaster** — emotional change in youth is violent: laughing until your stomach hurts one second, eyes red the next. Emotional switches between passages may be more abrupt than in other genres — that abruptness is itself the texture of youth
- **The growth arc is not a straight line** — a character's growth is not the straight line of "did not understand → understood", but the crooked line of "thought I understood → got slapped down → really understood". Every time they think they have grown up, they find out how far there is to go
- **The double line of memory and present** — coming-of-age narrative often uses the double-line structure of "looking back at the past from now". The memory line is full of vivid detail; the present line is what gives that detail the weight of "there is no going back"
- **The ensemble converging and parting** — the core narrative model of a coming-of-age ensemble is "coming together → living through it together → going separate ways". What makes people cry is not the parting itself, but "we once thought we would be together forever"
- **The "that one day" model** — every important turn in coming-of-age narrative should be bound to a concrete "that one day": the day it rained hard, the day of the sports meet, the day school let out early. A concrete anchor in time gives memory something to touch

## 3. Per-scene emotional design

### Key points for coming-of-age narrative

- **State the emotional target in the felt language of youth** — do not say "happy", say "laughing so hard she doubles over, then their eyes meet and she freezes". Do not say "sad", say "he only dares to cry once he is outside the school gate". The emotions of youth are always mixed and contradictory
- **Typical emotional passages and their design** —

| Passage type | Emotional direction | Narrative technique | Music suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Everyday fun | Energy + no worries | Ensemble horseplay, fragmented everyday life, fast cutting | Light rhythm, guitar / whistling / handclaps |
| A crush sprouting | Fluttering + nervous | Stealing glances, deliberately taking the same route, the electric shock of fingers touching | A gentle melody, the rhythm hinting at a heartbeat |
| Friendship tested | Hurt + stubborn | Misunderstanding, the silent treatment, eating lunch alone, pretending it is fine | Less scoring, ambience underlining the isolation |
| Striving / competing | Tense + fired up | The pressure of a countdown, teamwork, one last push | Drums building, rhythm accelerating |
| Farewell / graduation | Reluctance + release | Clearing out the drawer, walking together one last time, the signature book | From lively to quiet, until only one instrument is left |
| Looking back / grown | Nostalgia + warmth | Returning to the old place years later, opening an old keepsake, as if it were yesterday | Warm instrumental easing in, with a trace of desolation |

- **"Binding memory to space" is the core narrative tool** — in coming-of-age narrative a specific space should be bound to a specific emotion and recur to build emotional resonance:
  - **Classroom / sports field**: everyday life / collective memory / belonging
  - **The road home**: the space of the crush, of being alone, of things left unsaid
  - **The secret base** (the rooftop / under the tree / the old storeroom): friendship / promises / the space that is only ours
- **Season is emotion** — use the turn of the seasons to carry the passage of time and the change of mood: spring = sprouting / first meeting; summer = heat / conflict; autumn = parting / maturing; winter = settling / looking back
- **The point of view of "the person beside them"** — the advanced technique of coming-of-age narrative is "seeing the protagonist change through the person beside them" — the back of a figure a teacher sees through the window, the change in expression a friend quietly notices, the parent who realises their child no longer needs to be picked up

## 4. Sound and music direction

### Key points for coming-of-age narrative

- **Youth has its own soundscape** — the bell for class, trainers squeaking on the floor, pages turning, a whistle from the distant sports field, the click of a bicycle chain. These sounds make up "the sonic texture of youth"; every scene should mark 1-2 signature youth sound effects
- **Music is a memory anchor** — theme music has a special function in coming-of-age narrative: when the same melody returns in a different passage, the audience automatically retrieves the earlier emotional memory. Use different arrangements of the same theme in the key passages
- **The silence of growing up** — the moment a character truly "grows up" is usually silent: no longer arguing, no longer chasing, standing still and watching someone go. Strip the scoring from these growth beats and leave only ambience
- **Ambience is the period** — use ambience to carry the period setting: the exercise broadcast in the classroom, a pop song from a shop on the street, cicadas in summer. Sound raises the resonance of a period better than picture does
- **Music-emotion progression model** —

| Emotional stage | Music strategy | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday fun | Light rhythm, energetic instrumental | Medium-high |
| Undercurrent of feeling | Gentle melody, low volume | Medium-low |
| Conflict breaking out | Music stops dead, or an urgent rhythm | Extreme |
| Reconciliation, warming | A warm variation on the theme melody | Medium |
| Farewell, exit | From a group ensemble down to a single instrument | Medium→low |

- **The distance between laughter and crying** — the most moving sound treatment in coming-of-age narrative is "laughter suddenly turning into sobbing" or "crying until it turns into laughing". A fast emotional switch needs no scoring to help it; the natural human voice is enough

## 5. Composition and shot-size narrative

### Key points for coming-of-age narrative

- **The narrative function of the three core compositions** —
  - **Ensemble composition** — several people in frame, side by side or sitting in a circle, carrying the belonging and collective memory of "we are together"
  - **Foreground-background composition** — one in front and one behind, carrying the watching of a crush: "I am looking at you and you do not know"
  - **Symmetrical composition** — two people walking side by side or sitting on the same bench, carrying "companionship between equals"
- **Shot size follows the relationship** — when the characters first meet the shot sizes are wide and scattered (each in a different part of frame); once the relationship warms the shot sizes tighten (same frame / 近景 (medium close-up)); when they part the shot sizes pull back (拉远) again (cutting between each of them in 远景 (long shot))
- **The emotional memory of a space** — when the same space recurs across different passages, the composition should shift subtly to carry the emotional change: the same road home, from two walking abreast to one walking alone; the same classroom seat, from full to empty
- **The double function of 远景** — in coming-of-age narrative 远景 can carry "freedom / vastness" (running on the sports field, looking out from the rooftop) and equally "smallness / loneliness" (one person on an empty street after school)
- **The coming-of-age function of 大特写** — 大特写 (extreme close-up) catches the micro-expressions of youth that vanish in an instant: the corner of a mouth stifling a smile, an ear tip gone red, lashes blinking away tears
- **Anchoring the point of view of "that one day"** — the composition of a memory passage may deliberately take "the point of view of the memory": an imperfect angle, a partial obstruction, as if it were glimpsed in secret from some particular spot

## 6. Camera movement and rhythm

### Key points for coming-of-age narrative

- **Alternating movement and stillness is the rhythm of youth** — the shot rhythm of coming-of-age narrative should switch frequently between "energetic motion" and "quiet gazing". Running → stopping suddenly → looking back: that rhythm is itself youth
- **Tracking a run = the signature camera movement of youth** — following a character running (after a bus, after a person, in a race, in a sprint) is the most recognisable camera movement in coming-of-age narrative. Keep the tracking slightly unsteady to carry the life force of "going forward with everything you have"
- **缓推 (slow push in) = noticing / discovering** — the psychological cue of "having noticed that person"; good for a crush, for finding a friend crying, for noticing that something has changed
- **Slow pull back = leaving / growing up** — the psychological cue of "leaving this place or this person"; good for farewells, for graduation, for the realisation that everything is already different
- **静止 gazing = the moment of growth** — at the moment a character truly grows up the camera should stop and watch quietly. No push, no pull, no pan, just watching this person change in this instant
- **Camera movement is emotional temperature** — shaky tracking = energy / chaos; steady slow moves = warmth / everyday life; 静止 (static) = reflection / growth; a slow 拉远 = farewell / release
