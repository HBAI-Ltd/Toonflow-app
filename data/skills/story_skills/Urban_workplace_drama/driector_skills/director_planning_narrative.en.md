# Narrative technique · Urban Workplace Drama · technique reference

---

## 1. Theme, premise and the workplace core

### Key points for urban workplace narrative

- **Authenticity is the foundation** — the first duty of workplace narrative is to be believable. The audience checks every detail against their own experience, and any falseness throws them out. Scenes, dialogue and character behaviour must all survive the scrutiny of people who work
- **The undercurrent of power** — workplace conflict is not a street fight; it is the contest for the floor in a meeting room, the politics of who is CC'd on an email, an alliance or a betrayal exchanged in a single look. Cordial on the surface, churning underneath
- **The price of growth** — workplace growth is not levelling up by killing monsters; every step up carries a price: compromising a principle, losing a friend, bearing loneliness, doubting yourself. The growth arc is not a straight rise, it is a spiral
- **Suggested directions for the exit feeling** — recognition / reflection / being spurred on / mixed feelings. Avoid directions that do not match workplace realism, such as "thrill", "release" or "gratification"
- **The light of the small person** — the most moving workplace narrative is not "the big boss turning the world over with one hand", it is the small light of an ordinary person staying kind, staying professional, staying the course between the rules and the pressure

## 2. Narrative structure and the rhythm of manoeuvring

### Key points for urban workplace narrative

- **Chains of interest drive the narrative** — the driving force of a workplace story is not "feeling" but "interest". Every character's behaviour should have a clear motive of interest (promotion, holding a position, resources, face, security); the more concrete the motive, the more authentic the narrative
- **Asymmetric information builds tension** — in the workplace, "who knows what" decides the shape of power. The narrative should manage the information gaps between characters and between characters and audience with care: the audience knows about the betrayal and the protagonist does not = anxiety; the audience and the protagonist are kept in the dark together = empathy
- **Attack and defence changing hands** — the rhythm of workplace manoeuvring is attack and defence changing hands: the protagonist has the upper hand → the opponent strikes back → the protagonist is on the back foot → looks for an opening → turns the table. Every change of hands should have a definite "turning event" (an email, a leak, a decision)
- **Use everyday life between passages as breathing** — insert fragments of everyday life (the commute, a meal, time alone) between high-intensity passages of manoeuvring, so the audience breathes along with the character while the real texture of a working person's life is shown
- **Wins and losses along the way** — do not save the outcome for the end. Place one "small win" or "small loss" in every middle passage, so the audience feels progress and risk existing together
- **Leave an aftertaste at the end** — the ending of a workplace narrative should not be too neat. Even if the protagonist wins, leave the marks of what it cost (who was lost, what was compromised, the relationship that cannot be gone back to); the authenticity comes from the mixed feelings

## 3. Per-scene tension design

### Key points for urban workplace narrative

- **Describe tension through behaviour** — do not say "nervous", say "the pen cap clicked three times without noticing". A concrete behavioural detail is more convincing than an emotion label
- **Typical workplace passages and their design** —

| Passage type | Emotional direction | Narrative technique | Music suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Everyday work | Flat + real | Fragmentary everyday life: commuting, meetings, keys being hit | City white noise at very low volume, or no score |
| Manoeuvring undercurrent | Calm surface + undercurrent | Eyes crossing in a meeting, an email exchange in voiceover | Low-frequency rhythmic floor noise, building |
| Conflict breaks out | Confrontation + pressure | A head-on stand-off, laying the cards down, being exposed | Score cuts out, or cold strings |
| Alone / breaking down | Exhausted + real | Sitting alone in the car after work, washing the face late at night | Pure ambience (running water / the city in the distance) |
| Turn / breakthrough | Cool + resolute | Finding the key information, making the decision, striking back | A spare rhythmic score easing in |
| Reconciliation / the price | Release + complicated | A handshake with distance still held in the eyes, loneliness after the promotion | Warm but restrained instrumental |

- **Social distance is an expression of power** — map power relations onto the physical distance between characters:
  - **Superior**: free to come close to a subordinate at will (invading their space); the subordinate holds the distance (deference / fear)
  - **Peers manoeuvring**: seated opposite each other, a table's width apart (the manoeuvring distance)
  - **Close allies**: seated side by side, walking shoulder to shoulder (the same direction = the same camp)
- **Space is power** — use the office space to carry power relations. For example: a private office vs an open desk = rank; a seat position in the meeting room = who holds the floor; where people stand in the lift = the social order
- **Write shot intent as "the power relation", not "who is being shot"** — "the low angle is there so the audience feels the superior pressing down on the protagonist in this moment" beats "shoot the superior speaking from a low angle"

## 4. Sound and music direction

### Key points for urban workplace narrative

- **City white noise is the base coat of the atmosphere** — keys being struck, the coffee machine, the hum of the lift, a phone ringing somewhere off — these sounds make up the "smell" of the workplace and put the audience into the scene at once
- **Silence is the most sophisticated manoeuvre** — a sudden silence in a meeting, the pause after a question, saying nothing while holding a look — silence is a weapon in the workplace. In key manoeuvring passages, drop the score first and leave only ambience and silence
- **Score with extreme restraint** — keep total score coverage under 40% of the film. Workplace narrative chases authenticity, and too much score makes the picture look like an inspirational advertisement rather than a real story
- **Ambience separates the scenes** — mark 1-2 core ambient sounds for each scene to help tell the spaces apart: the office (keys + the hum of air conditioning), the meeting room (quiet + paper being turned), the commute (city noise), home late at night (the fridge humming + cars in the distance)
- **Music-emotion model** —

| Emotional stage | Music strategy | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday work | No score, pure ambience | None |
| Manoeuvring undercurrent | Low-frequency rhythmic floor noise | Low |
| Conflict and stand-off | Cold strings or total silence | Medium |
| Alone and real | Pure ambience or very low piano | Very low |
| Turn and breakthrough | A spare rhythmic score | Medium |
| The aftertaste of the ending | Restrained warm instrumental | Medium-low |

- **The narrative function of phone and message alerts** — a phone vibrating, a notification tone, a ringtone can be the trigger of a "turn of fate" in workplace narrative — one message changes the whole situation. Give these sound effects enough distinctiveness

## 5. Composition and shot-size narrative

### Key points for urban workplace narrative

- **The narrative function of the three core compositions** —
  - **The diagonal of power** — place the one holding power high or forward in frame and the weaker party low or behind; the diagonal layout implies an unequal power relation
  - **The trap of the frame** — a doorframe, a window frame, the border of a screen, a cubicle partition boxing the character in, carrying the pressure of "trapped inside the system"
  - **Symmetrical facing seats** — the two sides of the manoeuvre seated on either side of frame; symmetrical composition implies an evenly matched stand-off (or an apparent equality hiding intent)
- **The ceremony of centred composition** — save centred composition for the key "verdict" moments: the promotion announcement, the redundancy notice, the final decision. Not for everyday moments
- **The separation of glass and reflection** — the workplace is full of glass (meeting rooms, office doors, lifts). Shooting a character through glass creates the separation of "visible but out of reach", implying that workplace relations are transparent on the surface and walled underneath
- **The pressure of vertical space** — looking down from a tower, the lift rising and falling, going up and down the stairs — movement in vertical space maps directly onto "up and down the social ladder" in the workplace
- **Shot size is social distance** —
  - **远景 (long shot) / 全景 (wide shot)** — the whole of the space, the smallness of a person inside a vast institution
  - **中景 (medium shot)** — formal social distance, the standard shot size for meetings and dialogue
  - **近景 (medium close-up)** — social distance broken: pressure / intimacy / confrontation
  - **特写 (close-up)** — catching the micro-expression: the restrained smile at the corner of the mouth, the evasive look, the restless fingers
- **The psychological function of 大特写** — a pen snapped in two, a message on a phone screen, the tip of a pen trembling slightly as a signature is made. What 大特写 (extreme close-up) magnifies in the workplace is "the detail of the pressure"

## 6. Camera movement and rhythm

### Key points for urban workplace narrative

- **Cool and 固定 above all** — 65% or more of the shots should be on a tripod-mounted 固定 (locked off) camera, carrying the documentary texture of "cool observation". Workplace narrative needs no showy camera work; the authenticity comes from restraint
- **Documentary handheld** — use a light handheld when following a character walking (corridors, the commute, hurrying somewhere) to add the reality of "being there". Keep the shake extremely restrained, almost imperceptible
- **缓推 (slow push in) = pressure / scrutiny** — pushing slowly in towards a character's face simulates the pressure of "being stared at". It suits passages of being questioned, being tested, making a choice
- **Slow pull back = isolation / the whole picture** — pulling slowly back from the character to 全景, revealing how small and alone the character is in a huge office space. It suits passages of being alone, of failure, of working late into the night
- **Lateral move = scanning / running alongside** — a horizontal lateral move from one character to another, establishing the spatial relations. It suits scanning a meeting room, or an ensemble across an open-plan floor
- **Camera movement is narrative attitude** — 静止 (static) = cool observation; 缓推 = closing in / pressure; slow pull back = detachment / reflection; handheld tracking = real participation; lateral move = scanning the whole
- **Fast-cut montage** — in workplace narrative, fast cutting is used only for the "passage of time montage" (fragments of the same work day after day, snatches of an all-nighter on a project), never for everyday narrative
