# Live-Action Urban Visual Manual · Style Guide

This manual is built specifically for "live-action urban" themed visual work. All art prompts, visual specifications, and generated content are strictly limited to a live-action, photographic-grade urban film/TV aesthetic system.

---

## I. Core Style Definition

**Live-Action Urban** is a visual style system benchmarked against **live-action film/TV photography aesthetics**, centered on **contemporary Chinese cities**. Its core characteristics are:

- **Live-action photographic texture**: Anchored to film/TV-grade live-action photography, the image renders real people's skin texture, hair detail, micro-expressions, and body language, strictly excluding any sense of 3D modeling, CG rendering, or hand-drawn illustration.
- **Contemporary urban world**: All scenes are strictly confined to contemporary Chinese urban spaces — office towers, residential neighborhoods, streets and alleys, commercial districts, subways, parks, campuses, hospitals, and other authentic urban-life settings.
- **Cinema-level photographic language**: Benchmarked against theatrical-release live-action cinematography, emphasizing the organic interplay of natural and artificial light, professional narrative composition, emotional expression through depth of field and focus, and cinematic color grading.
- **Urban human warmth**: While maintaining professional imaging quality, the style emphasizes the everyday texture of city life, authentic emotional connection between people, and an aesthetic distillation of ordinary moments.

### Creative Philosophy and Core Values

The core creative philosophy of this manual is **"authenticity is power"**:

1. **The aesthetic return of authenticity**: As AI-generated imagery becomes increasingly ubiquitous, live-action realism — with its irreplaceable power of authenticity — has become the most emotionally penetrating visual language. Skin texture, the warmth in someone's eyes, the folds of clothing, the scattered light of sun through hair strands — these authentic details form a texture of life that 3D rendering cannot replicate in aesthetic depth.

2. **The city as narrative vessel**: The contemporary city is the narrative space Chinese people know best. The cubicles of office towers carry workplace dreams, the stairwells of old residential blocks echo with the bustle of everyday life, the eye contact between strangers in a subway car, the single light of a late-night convenience store — the urban space itself is a vast narrative vessel waiting to be explored.

3. **A humanist visual stance**: This manual holds firmly to "the person" as its core. However grand the scene or however dazzling the light and shadow, everything ultimately returns to the character's emotional state and inner world. Every frame is an act of looking at humanity itself.

4. **The critical point between cinema and the everyday**: The style seeks a subtle balance between "the polish of cinema" and "the truth of the everyday" — images beautiful enough, professional enough, and cinematic enough, without being so polished they lose their sense of life; characters authentic enough and vivid enough, without being so casual they lose aesthetic quality.

**Core values:**

- Provide AI visual creators with a systematic, reusable prompt methodology for live-action urban themes
- Establish a standardized visual creation pipeline from concept to finished output
- Lower the technical barrier and trial-and-error cost of AI visual creation for live-action urban themes
- Fill the current gap in the AI visual creation field for a "systematic methodology for live-action urban themes"

## III. Visual Style Positioning

### 3.1 Overall Style Positioning

**"Cinema-level live-action urban realism aesthetic"**

Using the contemporary Chinese city as its narrative stage and live-action film/TV photography as its technical anchor, the style pursues the image quality and aesthetic height of a theatrical-release urban film. Overall, the style seeks a golden balance between "cinematic feel" and "sense of everyday life" — images that are polished but not hollow, authentic but not careless, aesthetically sharp but not showy.

### 3.2 Image Texture Tiers

| Texture dimension | Positioning standard |
|---|---|
| **Skin texture** | Live-action-photography grade, showing natural skin texture, pores, fine lines, and moles/marks, strictly excluding excessive skin-smoothing and a plastic look |
| **Hair texture** | Individually distinct hair strands, naturally full or falling, with clear layering between lit and shadowed sides, strictly excluding stiff hair-block shapes |
| **Clothing texture** | Discernible fabric texture (the grain of cotton/linen, the fuzz of wool, the sheen of silk, the grain of leather), with folds that follow body dynamics and gravity |
| **Environment texture** | Walls show signs of wear, floors show natural wear, glass shows reflection and refraction, metal shows oxidation and scratches — strictly excluding a "brand-new prop" look |
| **Light-and-shadow texture** | Organic interplay of natural and artificial light, strictly excluding harsh, jarring contrast and unnatural noise, pursuing cinema-level color science |

## IV. Key Visual Elements

### 4.1 Character Visual Elements

Character visuals for live-action urban themes center on **"authentic individuals, urban temperament"**:

- **Facial standard**: Live-action skin texture, naturally three-dimensional features, makeup that suits urban aesthetics without over-styling. Supports diverse facial types — cool and intellectual, sunny and energetic, mature and composed, sharp and capable, gentle and healing, and more.
- **Hairstyle standard**: Contemporary urban trend hairstyles, natural hair texture, strictly excluding a wig-like feel or stiff styling. Supports long straight hair, collarbone-length hair, crisp short hair, tousled curls, low commute ponytails, and other urban hairstyles.
- **Clothing standard**: A contemporary urban everyday-dress system — commute formalwear (suits, shirts, trench coats), casual everyday wear (hoodies, knitwear, jeans), urban trend fashion (design-forward pieces, layered styling), and loungewear (comfortable knit homewear). Fabric texture should be clear, with natural folds.
- **Expression and posture**: Micro-expressions that are authentic and nuanced (in the eyes, the corners of the mouth, the brow), body language that is natural and unforced, strictly excluding a stiff, posed feel or excessive theatricality.
- **Character temperament dimensions**: Elite and capable / gentle and healing / youthful and energetic / mature and reserved / sharp and independent / down-to-earth and unpretentious / literary and melancholic

### 4.2 Scene Visual Elements

- **Office towers/offices**: Glass curtain walls, open-plan workstations, private offices, meeting rooms, break rooms — the transparency and separation of glass partitions, cool diffuse screen light, striped shadow patterns from afternoon sun through blinds
- **City streets**: Old streets and alleys, bustling commercial districts, overpasses and underpasses, bus stops, subway commutes — light and shadow through roadside trees, motion-blurred traffic, the sense of strangers flowing past, the urban texture of street signs and shop windows
- **Domestic spaces**: Old-neighborhood stairwells, high-rise apartment balconies, living rooms/kitchens/bedrooms — the warmth of window light pouring in, the lived-in traces of household clutter, the sense of air in fluttering curtains
- **Dining spaces**: Neighborhood noodle shops, trendy cafes, late-night convenience stores, night-market food stalls, company cafeterias — the bustle of food, condensation on glass, the enveloping feel of warm-toned lighting
- **Urban public spaces**: Park benches, hospital corridors, school playgrounds, bookstore corners, rooftop terraces — the way the space echoes the character's emotional state

### 4.3 Prop and Detail Elements

- **Urban-life props**: Phone (notification pop-ups, screen glare), laptop (keyboard glow on the face), coffee cup (rising steam), earphones (a symbol of solitude), umbrella (the core prop for rain scenes), employee badge, food-delivery packaging
- **Light-and-shadow props**: Desk lamp, street lamp, car headlights, neon signage, screen light source, candle — all serve as mood-makers in urban narrative
- **Emotional-symbol props**: Old photographs, letters, rings, tickets, lunch boxes, potted plants — small objects that carry narrative function
- **Seasonal and weather elements**: Spring cherry blossoms/catkins, summer downpour/cicada song, autumn falling leaves/trench coats, winter breath-fog/snowflakes — the sense of season deepens the temporal dimension of urban narrative

### 4.4 Cinematography Elements

| Shot size | Narrative function | Live-action urban characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| Extreme close-up | Emotional flashpoint, key detail | Eyes, a slight finger movement, a teardrop, a message on screen |
| Close-up | Character emotion, dialogue reaction | Facial micro-expression, shoulders and above, emphasizes emotional transmission |
| Medium close-up | Character interaction, action narrative | Waist and above, balances expression and gesture |
| Medium shot | Character relationship, spatial context | Full body in frame, the relationship between character and environment |
| Wide shot | Environmental atmosphere, character solitude/belonging | Character occupies a small portion of frame, primarily spatial narrative |
| Empty shot | Emotional buffer, metaphorical negative space | No characters, a pure environment shot, city landscape |

## V. Strictly Forbidden Content (highest priority)

This style strictly excludes the following content types, to keep the visual system pure and unified:

1. **Non-live-action texture**: 3D rendering / CG modeling / next-gen game-engine look / animation style / hand-drawn illustration / watercolor or oil painting / anime style / voxel / low-poly, and any other non-live-action-photographic image texture
2. **Non-urban world**: Ancient style / period costume / traditional Chinese classical architecture / xianxia / xuanhuan fantasy / wuxia / Republican-era China / Western fantasy / medieval / cyberpunk / sci-fi future / interstellar space, and any other non-contemporary-urban backdrop
3. **Non-contemporary-Chinese context**: Architecture, streets, signage, or character styling that clearly reads as non-Chinese-urban (e.g., kimono, Korean hanok, European small towns)
4. **Technical quality defects**: Low resolution / blur / noise / distortion / malformed limbs / distorted facial features / disproportionate scale / cut-out look / plastic look / excessive skin-smoothing / mask-like face
5. **Aesthetic-tendency violations**: Excessive high saturation / dead fluorescent color / cheap-filter look / excessive HDR / dirty, cluttered image / deliberately eerie color tone
6. **Content-compliance red lines**: Exposure / vulgarity / suggestiveness / graphic violence or gore / illegal content / discriminatory expression / politically sensitive elements

## VII. Usage Instructions

### Prompt Usage Principles

1. **Prefix first**: All generated content must first load the global style-anchoring segment from the "prefix prompt," locking in the live-action urban realism baseline
2. **Layer specialized prompts on top**: Building on the global prefix, layer on character/scene/prop/storyboard specialized prompts as needed
3. **Negative prompt required**: Every generation must include the "negative prompt" to avoid artifacts and style drift
4. **Iterative refinement**: After the first generation round, it's recommended to fine-tune the specialized prompts based on the result, rather than modifying the global prefix
5. **Platform adaptation**: This manual's prompts are optimized first for Doubao-Seedream (image generation) and Doubao-Seedance (video generation); some prompts may need minor adjustment depending on platform characteristics
