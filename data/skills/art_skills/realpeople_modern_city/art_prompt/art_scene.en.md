# Live-Action Urban Scene Image Generation · Constraint Manual

---

## 1. Scene aesthetic principles

> A live-action urban scene is not "a modelled space" but "a real place the camera has chosen". No render engine, no textures, no volumetric light parameters — only light that actually exists, real materials, and the everyday life of a Chinese city.

1. **Space is narrative** — an empty office is not "empty", it is just after work. A chair pushed askew, a half-cold coffee, a document still open on the screen — the space tells its own story
2. **Real light, not rendered light** — the light in a scene has only one origin: actual daylight (through windows, doorways) and actual lamps (fluorescent tubes, desk lamps, street lights, neon signs). The colour temperature and direction of the light must be physically defensible
3. **A space that has been used** — a real scene is not tidy. There is clutter on the desk, dust in the corners, handprints on the glass, wear on the floor. A space with no trace of life at all is a show flat, not live-action urban
4. **A real place in a contemporary Chinese city** — the scene must have Chinese signage and Chinese urban markers (shop signs, road signs, architectural style, street trees). This is not "generic urban", this is "one specific corner of a contemporary Chinese city"
5. **A single full-view image of the whole space** — one photograph holding the complete information of the entire location. Wide-angle or standard focal length, deep focus, showing the whole space and its depth head-on — not a split four-view sheet, not a multi-angle collage

## 2. Season and time of day — the director of natural light

> The light of a live-action urban scene is decided first by season and time of day. Below is the photographic behaviour of real light sources across four seasons and five time bands.

| Season | Time band | Behaviour of the light | Scene atmosphere words |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spring | Early morning 6:00-8:00 | Low-angle warm white daylight, diffusion from thin mist in the air, building surfaces soft | Clear and crisp, new green just opening, morning mist not yet gone |
| Spring | Midday 11:00-13:00 | Daylight almost vertical, short shadows, building surface materials clear, light cool-white | Bright and fresh, spring in bloom, the block radiant |
| Spring | Afternoon 14:00-16:00 | Daylight moving west, warm tone gradually strengthening, long shadows starting to form | Languid and warm, afternoon light and shadow, spring warmth spreading |
| Spring | Golden hour 17:00-18:30 | Warm gold low-angle light, long shadows, every surface gilded at the edge, the most precious natural light | Golden spring light, warm and healing, time running slow |
| Spring | Night 19:00-next day | Street lights dominant, shop-window light secondary, cool and warm light coexisting | Spring night slightly cool, street lamps just lit, the night gentle |
| Summer | Early morning 5:00-7:00 | Very early warm light, clear air, long shadows, the city not fully awake yet | Summer morning light, clear air, before the city wakes |
| Summer | Midday 11:00-14:00 | Strong top light, shadows dense and short, strong reflection off building surfaces, faint heat haze on the road | Height of summer under a fierce sun, deep shade blocking the sun, the stillness of a cicada-loud afternoon |

## 3. Interior scenes

### Spatial photography specification

| Dimension | Photographic requirement | Prompt |
|---|---|---|
| Style | A real contemporary Chinese urban interior — not an architectural visualisation, not a show flat, not a render. A real interior with traces of life | a real contemporary Chinese urban {space type}, live-action photography, not a visualisation not a show flat |
| Light-source logic | There must be a clear origin for the light — which window the daylight comes through, which lamp the artificial light is. The direction and colour temperature of the light must be self-consistent | {window light direction} natural light dominant, {artificial light source} secondary, light direction clear and traceable |
| Spatial layers | A single full-view photograph must show foreground/midground/background — the foreground (door frame/edge of furniture/curtain) forms a frame within the frame, the midground is the main body of the space, the background is the space extending on (another room/outside the window/the end of the corridor) | foreground {framing element}, midground {main body of the space}, background {extension of the space}, wide-angle lens capturing the full depth of the space |
| Materials and surfaces | Walls carry traces of being lived in (stains/sticker residue/nail holes), the floor has normal wear, furniture surfaces have the patina of use — every material appears under the camera, not rendered | real traces of use on the walls, normal floor wear, patina of use on furniture surfaces, not brand new not a show flat |
| Lens choice | 24-28mm wide angle — holding the full breadth of the space, the depth from foreground to background all sharp (deep focus f/8-f/11), slight barrel distortion adding a sense of space | wide angle 24-28mm, deep focus f/8-f/11, full spatial depth, slight perspective adding a sense of space |

## 5. Single-view full-view photography specification

> A live-action urban scene needs only a **single** full-view photograph — one photograph holding the complete information of the whole space. Not a 2×2 four-view sheet, not a multi-angle assembly.

### Frame composition

| Item | Photographic requirement |
|---|---|
| Spatial presentation | A single photograph shows the full spatial extent of the scene. Use 24-28mm wide angle or 35mm; the depth, width and height of the whole location must all be visible in one frame |
| Composition | Deep focus, wide shot size — the foreground (door frame/furniture/railing/trees/window frame) forms a natural frame → the midground is the main body of the location → the background extends into the depth of the space or out of the window/to the skyline |
| Depth of field | Deep focus f/8-f/11, everything sharp front to back. This is not portrait photography — every corner of the scene should be visible |
| People | **No people, human shadows or human silhouettes may appear at all.** The scene is empty — but this "emptiness" carries narrative: just after work/about to begin/everyone has moved out/the still moment of ordinary life |
| Aspect ratio | 16:9 or 3:2 wide format recommended — matching the horizontal viewing habit of full-view spatial presentation |
| Colour | Driven by the colour temperature of the actual light sources; do not apply a filter. Colour science referencing ARRI Alexa/Sony Venice cinema tonality |

### How an empty scene tells a story

| Type of "emptiness" | Clues in the frame | Narrative implication |
|---|---|---|
| Just after work | the monitor is dark but the power light is on, the chair has not been pushed back in, a half-finished glass of water on the desk | Someone was here a moment ago and has now gone |
| About to begin | chairs set out, tabletop tidy, projector already on, curtains drawn, bottled water already placed | Someone is about to arrive; this is the stillness before the start |
| Everyone has moved out | an empty room after moving day, marks left by hooks and nails on the wall, outlines of dust on the floor | Someone once lived here and has now moved away |
| The stillness of ordinary life | a blanket thrown casually over the sofa, the remote and a half-drunk cup of tea on the coffee table, light from outside falling on the floor | The normal state of living, only with nobody in frame at this moment |
| A place late at night | an empty corridor with only the fluorescent lights on, the quiet between convenience-store shelves, the platform after the last train | The city has fallen asleep, but the space is still awake |

## 7. Constraint rules

### Mandatory

| No. | Rule |
|---|---|
| R1 | Must be a **single** full-view photograph — single view, not a 2×2 four-view sheet, not multi-angle, not a turnaround |
| R2 | Must use a wide-angle lens (24-28mm) or a standard focal length (35mm), deep focus f/8-f/11, complete spatial depth |
| R3 | The light source must be traceable — the direction of natural light matching the time of day, the position and colour temperature of artificial light sources reasonable |
| R4 | The scene must have foreground/midground/background spatial layers: foreground frame → midground main body → background extension |
| R5 | Must be recognisable as a contemporary Chinese city — Chinese signage/Chinese architectural style/Chinese urban markers |
| R6 | Must carry traces of use — natural wear on walls/floor/furniture, not a show flat, not brand new, not a visualisation |
| R7 | **No people, human shadows, human silhouettes or outlines may appear at all** |
| R8 | Must state the core anchor "live-action photography + not 3D rendered not CG" |

### Strictly forbidden

| No. | Strictly forbidden |
|---|---|
| X1 | Strictly forbidden: "3D render / 3D modelling / CG / UE engine / Blender / PBR material / volumetric light / AO" and every other CG term |
| X2 | Strictly forbidden: "2D hand-drawn / illustration / animation / anime" and other non-photographic media |
| X3 | Strictly forbidden: "four views / 2×2 grid / multiple angles / turnaround / orthographic view / blueprint" — a single image only |
| X4 | Strictly forbidden: "ancient style/antiquity/xianxia/wuxia/Republican era/cyberpunk/sci-fi/Western fantasy/other worlds" and other non-contemporary-urban scenes |
| X5 | Strictly forbidden: "an invented city with no Chinese identity / all-English signage" |
| X6 | Strictly forbidden: "any person/human shadow/human silhouette/outline/limb appearing" |
| X7 | Strictly forbidden: scenes that are "a show flat/a sales-office visualisation/brand new and unmarked/in perfect condition/with no trace of life" |
| X8 | Strictly forbidden: "light of unknown origin/globally even illumination/light with no direction" |
| X9 | Strictly forbidden: "blown-out whites/crushed blacks/no depth of field/a flat image" |
| X10 | Strictly forbidden: "watermark / text / LOGO / signature / border / traces of AI generation" |
