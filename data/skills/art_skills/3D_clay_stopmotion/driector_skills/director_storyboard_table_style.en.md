# Storyboard-Table Clay Constraints · Stop-Motion Clay · Technique Reference

---

## 1. Where the storyboard table sits

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into camera language. What follows provides only the constraint specification of the stop-motion clay style at storyboard-table level.

---

## 2. Lighting and atmosphere

- **One lighting scheme per scene** — no more than two lighting schemes should appear within a single scene, unless there is an explicit narrative turn (for example switching off a desk lamp → the cold sheen of moonlight)
- **Emotion-palette binding** — every scene binds at least 1 emotional scene (such as a heart-flutter meeting/everyday warmth/parting sorrow) and keeps the main + secondary colors continuous within the shot group
- **Light-source angle specification** — 30-45° side light by default, simulating the effect of small-theatre lighting. When describing the lighting, distinguish the angle difference between front-lit and side-lit
- **Match warm/cool tone to the narrative stage** — the first half (everyday/work/study) keeps warm tones; cool tones (cool blue + warm contrast) are used at night or after an emotional turn
- **Lighting transition is an advanced device** — gradating from natural light (A) to warm interior light (C) = the passage of time from day to night. Mark the lighting change points in the storyboard table

---

## 3. Environmental dynamics

- **Environmental motion gives the frame room to breathe** — leaves stirring, candle flames flickering, curtains swaying, raindrops falling, magic light motes. Every 3-4 shots, schedule at least one shot with environmental motion so the frame does not go "dead"
- **Clay-style environmental elements come first** — environmental motion should use elements from within the clay-style world: leaves stirring, candle flames flickering, curtains swaying, small props moving slightly, magic light motes, expressing the handcrafted texture and the motion of a miniature world

---

## 4. Clay-style action rhythm

- **Stop-motion action must be slow** — all character action defaults to a naturally slow rhythm. Standing up, turning around and raising a hand should all be marked "natural" or "gentle"
- **Clay-texture motion** — the action of clay characters carries a built-in "clumsy and cute" quality; small movements such as a finger touching or a slight tilt of the head can be added to the shot description to make the frame feel more "alive"
- **Sweet-romance interaction is implied** — meeting gazes, fingers touching lightly, fingertips brushing when handing something over. Do not write large two-person interactions; break them into implied partial shots

---

## 5. Camera-movement taboos

- **Fast camera movement is banned** — whip pan (甩镜), abrupt push-in and fast switching clash with the temperament of the stop-motion clay style
- **Fancy transitions are banned** — wipe, rotation, venetian blind and the like are incompatible with this style
