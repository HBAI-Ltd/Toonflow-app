# Clay Constraints · Stop-Motion Clay · Technique Reference

---

## 1. Tonal system and visual keynote

- **Tonal base** — the whole film uses cream yellow (C1), terracotta red (C4) and sky blue (C6) as its base colors, with a warm overall color temperature (4500-5000K) and medium-low saturation (60-75%), giving the warm, nostalgic feel of retro stop-motion animation
- **Warm tone through the whole film** — the whole film keeps a warm tonal base, with local cool accents (pale purple C7, mint green C8) to add layering. Warm/cool switches should track the story arc, not be mixed at random
- **Palette-first principle** — passage planning must bind an emotional scene first (meeting/everyday/adventure/parting, etc.), then settle the main + secondary colors and the lighting scheme, to avoid "the plot is right but the emotion is in the wrong color"
- **Banned color range** — highly saturated fluorescent colors, neon colors and dark color systems are all incompatible with this style

---

## 2. Lighting scheme system

- **Lighting is narrative** — the 6 lighting schemes correspond to different emotional passages; in the planning stage the director should set the lighting keynote direction at passage level rather than shot by shot
- **Light-source angle** — 30-45° side light by default, simulating the effect of small-theatre lighting

| Lighting scheme | Scheme name | Tonal tendency | Applicable emotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Soft diffused light | Cream yellow base + even soft light | Everyday warmth |
| B | Whimsical warm light | Warm amber + magic light motes | Whimsical entrance |
| C | Warm side light | Warm yellow keynote + local warm light | Indoor everyday |
| D | Diffused daylight | Natural warm tone + layered shadows | Outdoor nature |
| E | Cool moonlight | Pale blue keynote + warm accents | Night dreamscape |
| F | Warm soft-focus light | Warm yellow keynote + hazing effect | Memory/flashback |

- **Warm tones through the whole film** — adjust flexibly as the scene requires; cool tones (pale blue + warm contrast) are used only in night/dream passages
- **Atmosphere direction mapping** — the atmosphere direction of every scene should map onto one of the lighting schemes above (A-F), to keep the visuals consistent

---

## 3. Texture direction

- **Documentary feel of stop-motion clay texture** — the core of stop-motion clay realism: fingerprint indentations visible, clay creases clear, obvious material graininess
- **Clay texture + delicate light and shadow** — this is the anchor of the visual texture. Not smooth plastic, not modern CG rendering, but the handcrafted texture of stop-motion animation
- **Material believability** — the material of every costume, makeup and prop must be believable: the graininess of clay, the creases of fabric, the grain of wood, the sheen of metal. A "plastic look" and a "smooth CG look" are forbidden
- **Clay does not mean crude** — this style emphasises the clay-texture expression of stop-motion animation (clear texture, fingerprint indentations, clay creases), amplifying its impact through lighting and action design rather than relying on complex effects

---

## 4. Clay-style scene space elements

Scene elements characteristic of the clay-style world and their visual narrative function:

- **Miniature sets/small props** — natural framing props for composition; small-scale sets create the distinctive layering and spatial depth of a "cute miniature world"
- **Warm interiors/handmade props** — the natural vehicle for negative-space composition; handcrafted props add a "real tactile feel": a wooden table = the warmth of home, a flowerpot = the breath of daily life, a book = knowledge/growth
- **Lamplight/window light/candlelight** — the light-source vehicles of the clay-style world: warm yellow lamplight = cozy/safe (schemes A/C), moonlight = dreamlike/cool-toned (scheme E), window light = everyday/natural (scheme D)
- **Use empty scene shots to bridge passages** — this style has rich scene assets (time-of-day/weather variants), so bridging passages with an empty scene shot as an emotional buffer is recommended; do not hard-cut
- **Use visuals at turning points, not dialogue** — prefer visual means (lighting change, a jump cut between shot sizes, empty-shot metaphor) together with action design, rather than relying on dialogue to explain

---

## 5. Clay-style instruments and ambient sound

Constraints on the sound elements of the clay-style world:

### Instrument choice

- **Music box** — the core instrument for everyday/cozy/memory passages; it best expresses the texture of stop-motion animation
- **Accordion** — for light/romantic/street passages; the tenderness of the strumming suits an everyday atmosphere
- **String bed** — for emotional-climax/parting passages; the delicacy of bowed strings suits emotional transitions
- **Xylophone/glockenspiel** — for whimsical/magic passages; the crisp timbre suits a dreamlike atmosphere
- A string bed can add a cinematic feel but must not steal the show

### Instrument combination strategy

| Emotional stage | Instrument combination |
|---|---|
| Steady/opening/ending | Xylophone solo or glockenspiel solo |
| Everyday warmth | Music box + accordion |
| Emotional climax/romance | Strings + music box |
| Steady emotion | Music box solo |

### Clay-style ambient sound

- **Typical ambient-sound layers** — footsteps (with clay texture) / pages turning / tableware clinking / wind / a clock ticking / fabric rustling
- **Label 1-2 core ambient sounds for every scene**, to help the later sound-effect design. The richer the ambient-sound layers, the more immersive the clay-style scene
