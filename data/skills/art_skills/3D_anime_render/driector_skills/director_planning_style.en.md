# 3D Anime Render Constraints · 3D Cartoon Rendering · Technique Reference

---

## 1. Tonal system and visual keynote

- **Tonal base** — the whole film uses warm orange (C1), sky blue (C3) and cherry-blossom pink (C5) as its base colors, with a warm overall color temperature (4800-5200K) and medium-high saturation (65-80%), giving the warm, healing feel of bright 3D anime
- **Warm/cool narrative contrast** — warm colors (warm orange C1, warm amber, warm pink) run through the whole film as the keynote, while cool colors (pale blue C3, pale purple C6) serve as local accents that add layering. Warm/cool switches should track the story arc, not be mixed at random
- **Warm/cool allocation strategy** — the whole film keeps a warm tonal base; cool tones are used only in night or emotional-turn passages, forming a warm/cool contrast
- **Palette-first principle** — passage planning must bind an emotional scene first (meeting/everyday/adventure/parting, etc.), then settle the main + secondary colors and the lighting scheme, to avoid "the plot is right but the emotion is in the wrong color"
- **Banned color range** — dull dark color ranges, over-saturated muddy colors and any color system that does not match the bright temperament of 3D anime are all incompatible

## 2. Lighting scheme system

- **Lighting is narrative** — the 6 lighting schemes correspond to different emotional passages; in the planning stage the director should set the lighting keynote direction at passage level rather than shot by shot
- **Light-source angle** — 45° side-backlight by default, simulating the effect of dusk sunset glow

| Lighting scheme | Scheme name | Tonal tendency | Applicable emotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Soft morning light | Warm yellow base + even soft light | Opening of the film, urban early morning |
| B | Warm side light | Warm orange keynote + local warm light | Everyday warmth, cafe/at home |
| C | Top light + ambient light | Neutral warm + soft cool white | Work/study passages |
| D | Backlit sunset glow | Warm amber dominant + warm pink accents | Dusk romance, sweet passages |
| E | Neon glow | Warm orange + cool blue background | Night street scenes, romantic passages |
| F | Warm soft-focus light | Warm yellow keynote + hazing effect | Memory/emotional passages |

- **Warm/cool light allocation** — warm tones may be used throughout the film; cool tones (cool blue + warm contrast) are used at night or after an emotional turn. The director may adjust the warm/cool switch points as the narrative requires
- **Atmosphere direction mapping** — the atmosphere direction of every scene should map onto one of the lighting schemes above (A-F), to keep the visuals consistent

## 3. Texture direction

- **Cel-shaded documentary feel** — the core of 3D animation rendering: clear outlines, bright coloring, soft light-and-shadow layering
- **High-detail materials + soft light-and-shadow** — this is the anchor of the visual texture. Not over-smoothed, not modern realistic rendering, but the cel-shaded texture characteristic of 3D anime
- **Rich material detail** — the material detail of all costumes, makeup and props should be believable: the crease grain of fabric, the cartoon highlight on metal, the warm sheen of food. "Over-realism" and a "coarse low-poly look" are forbidden
- **Cel shading is not flatness** — 3D anime emphasises cel-shaded expression (clear outlines, bright coloring, soft light-and-shadow layering), amplifying its impact through lighting and compositional design rather than relying on over-complex effects

## 4. Urban scene space elements

The scene elements characteristic of the 3D anime urban world and their visual narrative function:

- **Door and window frames/corridors/staircases** — natural framing props for composition, creating spatial depth and layering
- **Streets/parks/sky** — natural carriers of negative-space composition; the scene is the feeling: long shadows on a dusk street = romance, the warm light of a cafe = safety, a window view from a high floor = urban distance
- **Desk lamps/neon/window light** — the light-source carriers of the urban world; a desk lamp = warm/private (scheme B), neon = energy/romance (scheme E), window light = everyday/quiet (scheme A)
- **Use empty scene shots to bridge passages** — this style has rich scene assets (variants for different times of day/weather conditions/weather); to join passages, use an empty scene shot as an emotional buffer rather than a hard cut
- **Use frame + action at turning points** — prefer visual means (lighting change, scene change) paired with action design over relying on dialogue to explain

## 5. Urban instruments and ambient sound

Constraints on the sound elements of the 3D anime urban world:

### Instrument choice

- **Piano** — the core instrument for everyday/warm/memory passages; it best expresses the texture of 3D anime
- **Guitar** — for light/romantic/street passages; the gentleness of strumming suits the urban atmosphere
- **String bed** — for emotional-sublimation/parting passages; the delicacy of bowed strings suits emotional transitions
- **Electronic sounds** — for youthful/rhythmic passages; suits modern urban scenes
- Ambient sound is an important element but must not steal the show

### Instrument combination strategy

| Emotional stage | Instrument combination |
|---|---|
| Steady/opening/closing | Solo guitar or electronic sounds |
| Everyday warmth | Piano + light guitar |
| Emotional sublimation/romance | Strings + piano |
| Tense/urgent | Electronic sounds + string bed |

### Urban ambient sound

- **Typical ambient sound layers** — city traffic / cafe background sound / metro announcement chime / wind through leaves / crowd chatter / footsteps / elevator sound
- **Mark 1-2 core ambient sounds for every scene**, to help the later sound design. The richer the ambient sound layers, the more immersive the urban scene
