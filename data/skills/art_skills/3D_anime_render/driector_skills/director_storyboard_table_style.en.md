# Storyboard-Table 3D Anime Render Constraints · 3D Cartoon Rendering · Technique Reference

---

## 1. Where the storyboard table sits

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into camera language. What follows provides only the constraint specification of the 3D cartoon rendering style at storyboard-table level.

---

## 2. Lighting and atmosphere

- **One lighting scheme per scene** — no more than two lighting schemes should appear within a single scene, unless there is an explicit narrative turn (for example switching off a desk lamp → the cold sheen of moonlight)
- **Emotion-palette binding** — every scene binds at least 1 emotional scene (such as a heart-flutter meeting/everyday warmth/parting sorrow) and keeps the main + secondary colors continuous within the shot group
- **Light-source angle specification** — 45° side-backlight by default, simulating the effect of dusk sunset glow. When describing the lighting, distinguish the angle difference between front-lit and side-lit
- **Match warm/cool tone to the narrative stage** — the first half (everyday/work/study) keeps warm tones; cool tones (cool blue + warm contrast) are used at night or after an emotional turn
- **Lighting transition is an advanced device** — gradating from natural light (A) to warm interior light (B) = the passage of time from day to night. Mark the lighting change points in the storyboard table

---

## 3. Environmental dynamics

- **Environmental motion gives the frame room to breathe** — leaves stirring, clouds drifting, traffic passing, raindrops falling, lights flickering. Every 3-4 shots, schedule at least one shot with environmental motion so the frame does not go "dead"
- **Urban environmental elements come first** — environmental motion should use elements from within the urban world: leaves stirring, clouds drifting, traffic passing, neon flickering, curtains stirring; elements incompatible with the 3D anime style are forbidden

---

## 4. 3D anime action rhythm

- **Action must stay natural** — all character action defaults to a natural rhythm. Standing up, turning around and raising a hand should all be marked "natural" or "gentle"
- **Sweet-romance interaction is implied** — meeting gazes, fingers touching lightly, fingertips brushing when handing something over. Do not write large two-person interactions in the storyboard table; break them into implied partial shots
- **3D anime smoothness** — the "frame-to-frame smoothness" characteristic of 3D anime; action should be continuous and natural, avoiding stiff frame jumps

---

## 5. Camera-movement taboos

- **Fast camera movement is banned** — whip pan (甩镜), abrupt push-in and fast switching clash with the temperament of 3D cartoon rendering (unless it is a transition or an emphasis)
- **Fancy transitions are banned** — wipe, rotation, venetian blind and the like are incompatible with this style
