# Storyboard Table Ancient-Style Constraints · Live-Action Ancient Chinese Realism · Technique Reference

---

## 1. What the storyboard table is for

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning a script into shot language. What follows gives only the constraints of the live-action ancient Chinese realism style at the storyboard-table level.

---

## 2. Light and atmosphere

- **Unified lighting within one scene** — no more than two lighting schemes should appear within a single scene, unless there is an explicit narrative turn (such as candlelight being blown out → cold moonlight radiance)
- **Emotion palette binding** — every scene binds at least 1 emotional scene (such as first-sight flutter/separation and misunderstanding/reunion and release), and keeps the main + secondary colors continuous within the shot group
- **Light-source angle specification** — for a front-facing shot the light source is 38.5° raking by default (not a vague 45°); for a side-facing shot, adjust it to what the frame needs. When describing light, you must distinguish the angle difference between front-facing and side-facing shots
- **Match warm/cool tone to the narrative stage** — grief/isolation/abandonment passages strictly use cool light (indigo blue-gray, cool white), with warm orange/amber forbidden; the warm scheme (warm orange + cool blue background contrast) is used only after the feelings warm up again
- **Lighting transitions are an advanced device** — a gradual shift from light through a window gauze (E) to warm candlelight shadow (C) = the passage of time from day to night. Mark the lighting change points in the storyboard table

---

## 3. Environmental motion

- **Environmental motion gives the frame room to breathe** — falling petals, rising smoke, rippling water, drifting gauze curtains. Every 3-4 shots, place at least one shot with environmental motion so the frame does not go "dead"
- **Ancient-style environmental elements first** — environmental motion should use elements from within the ancient Chinese world: falling blossoms, drifting catkins, light smoke, running water, wind through a bamboo grove, flickering candle flames; modern elements are forbidden

---

## 4. Ancient-style action rhythm

- **Ancient-style action must be slow** — all character actions are slow by default. Standing up, turning around, raising a hand should all be marked "slow"
- **Clothing motion** — the drifting of ancient-style clothing (wide sleeves, skirt hems, shawls) is a natural motion asset; the motion of sleeves and skirt hems can be added to the shot description to make the frame feel "alive"
- **Bearing constraint** — the conduct of ancient-style characters should match the temperament of the era: walking steady and unhurried, gestures reserved and restrained, with no modernized body language (such as shrugging or throwing up the hands)

---

## 5. Camera-movement taboos

- **Fast camera movement is forbidden** — whip pan (甩镜), abrupt push-ins and handheld shake clash with the temperament of live-action ancient Chinese realism
- **Gimmick transitions are forbidden** — wipes, spins, venetian-blind transitions and the like are incompatible with this style
