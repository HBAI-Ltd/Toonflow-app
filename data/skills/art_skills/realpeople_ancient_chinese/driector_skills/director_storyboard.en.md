## Lighting and atmosphere word bank (live-action ancient Chinese realism)

> **Design principle**: the lighting of the frame is split into two layers, **ambient light (primary)** and **fixture light (secondary)**. Ambient light is determined by the time, weather and space of the scene and is the absolute keynote of the frame's lighting; fixture light is used only to model the character and reinforce emotion, must obey the direction and color temperature of the ambient light, and must not create the sense of an independent light source.

### 1. Ambient light (key light · natural light of the scene)

> Every storyboard shot **must settle the ambient light first**, then decide whether to add fixture light.

#### Ambient light by time of day

| Time of day | Natural light source | Tonal leaning | Ambient atmosphere |
|--------|---------|---------|----------|
| Early morning | Diffused morning light, diffused skylight | Cool white tone, pale blue tone | Thin mist spreading, a sense of air, the texture of morning dew |
| Afternoon | Raking sunlight, diffused scattered light | Neutral tone, lightly warm tone | Dappled light and shadow, clear layering |
| Evening/dusk | Raking afterglow, skylight alternating cool and warm | Mainly cool, local warm light at the horizon | Long shadows stretching, poetic light |
| Night | Cool blue moonlight, warm points of candle flame/lantern | Cool blue keynote | Deep dim light and shadow, strong light-dark contrast |
| Overcast rain | Diffused cool light, cloud cover with no key light | Gray cool tone | A damp sense of air, low saturation |

#### Ambient light by scene space

| Space type | Typical ambient light | Lighting character |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| Open exterior | Mainly skylight, sun direction decides light and dark | Aerial perspective in the distance forms layering naturally |
| Courtyard/garden | Skylight filtered through branches and leaves, dappled scattering | Shadow under the corridor and the lit area in the courtyard form light-dark contrast |
| Interior (window light) | One-directional natural light from the window, darkening deeper inside | Light rakes in from the side, forming a natural light-dark gradation |
| Secret room/dark room | Local warm light of candle flame/oil lamp, dark overall tone | Single light source, limited lit area, heavy shadow |
| Bamboo grove/wilderness | Light piercing through gaps, scattered light patches | Light coming through above, light and shadow interlacing on the ground |
| Water surface/lakeside | Skylight + reflection off the water | Shimmering ripples, reflections filling in light, a hazy quality |

### 2. Fixture light (fill light · character modeling)

> ⚠️ Fixture light **must obey the keynote of the ambient light**: its direction matches the ambient light source, its color temperature must not clash with the ambient light, and its intensity must not exceed the ambient light.

#### Fixture light types

| Light type | Function | Suitable scene | Usage constraint |
|----------|------|---------|----------|
| Rim light (edge light) | Outlines the character's edge, strengthens figure-ground separation | Backlit/side-backlit environments | Intensity lower than the ambient light, color temperature matching the ambient light |
| Catchlight | Adds spirit and life to the eyes | Close-up (特写)/medium close-up (近景) shots containing a character | Extremely faint, only a finishing touch, must not form a visible light spot |
| Fill light (bounce) | Lifts detail in dark areas, keeps the face from going dead black | When strong side light/backlight makes the dark areas too heavy | Simulates an environmental bounce, must not create the sense of an independent light source |
| Oppressive top light | Creates facial shadow, strengthens the sense of pressure | Interrogation, confrontation, majesty scenes | Must be justified by a light source in the scene (lantern, skylight, hall-ceiling light) |

#### Emotion → ambient light + fixture light pairing

| Emotional keynote | Ambient light (primary) | Fixture light (secondary) | Pairing principle |
|----------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| Heart-flutter/tenderness | Soft side-backlit natural light, diffused warm-toned environment | Rim light faintly outlining | Ambient light sets the side-backlit keynote, fixture light only adds the outline, shallow depth of field softens the background |
| Confrontation/pressure | Hard natural side light, high-contrast light-dark environment | Top light may be added (must have a justified source) | Ambient light sets the main direction and hardness, fixture light only strengthens contrast, shadows are hard-edged |
| Oppression/sorrow | Diffused cool light, overcast/dark-room environment | Very little fill, keep the dark areas of the face | Mainly cool ambient light, deliberately unfilled to keep the shadow layering |
| Mystery/solemnity | Cool blue ambient light, backlit silhouette | Edge light precisely controlled, halo restrained | Ambient light sets the cool blue keynote, fixture light only separates the edge |
| Ethereal/atmospheric | Diffused soft light, natural light slightly overexposed | Usually no fixture light needed | Mainly diffused ambient light, distance faint and thin, a sense of aerial perspective |

### 3. Rules for avoiding lighting conflicts

> When generating storyboard prompts, the following principles must be strictly followed so the lighting of the frame stays natural and believable:

1. **Settle the ambient light first, then layer on the fixture light**: for each storyboard shot, first settle the ambient-light keynote from "time of day + scene space", then decide whether fixture light is needed to model the character.
2. **Color-temperature consistency**: the fixture light's color temperature must be in harmony with the ambient light (for example, a cool blue night environment must not have warm yellow fill layered on it, unless there is a justified in-scene warm source such as a candle flame).
3. **Directional justification**: the direction of the fixture light must be supported by a light source in the scene (the direction of the window light, the position of the candle flame, the angle of the moonlight); it must not appear out of nowhere.
4. **Subordinate intensity**: the fixture light's intensity **must not exceed the ambient light**, avoiding the studio feel of "the character lit on their own".
5. **Better none than too much**: when the ambient light is already enough to model the character and express the emotion, **add no fixture light**.

**Live-action realism anchor (required):**
```
Live-action realistic photography, cinematic image quality, hyperrealist documentary, high contrast, extreme detail, ultra-crisp texture
```

**Character texture (required when the shot contains a character):**
```
Delicate skin, delicate facial rendering, three-dimensional features, hair strands separated one by one, delicate hair-strand rendering
```

**Clothing texture (required when the shot contains a character):**
```
Clear texture of the clothing fabric, ultra-crisp texture detail, true fabric creases, clothing drifting with the movement
```

**Consistency anchor (required in reference-image mode):**
```
Keep the character's face consistent with the reference image, keep the clothing colors consistent with the reference image, keep the scene lighting style unified
```

**Style closing (fixed):**
```
Ancient-style realist aesthetics, Eastern classical charm, cinematic storyboard composition
```

## Aesthetic prohibitions (avoid strictly when generating)

The following words/styles must not appear in the output prompt:

- ❌ Japanese/Korean fresh-filter words (such as: fresh, dainty-fresh, Japanese feel)
- ❌ Words relating to anime/2D anime/illustration/CG rendering
- ❌ Modern clothing/modern scene elements
- ❌ Warm-yellow keynote tonal words (you may use "local warm candlelight accents")
- ❌ Soft-focus/hazy/low-contrast filter words
- ❌ Clashing colors/mash-ups/neon/fluorescent color families
- ❌ Deformation descriptions such as cartoon proportions, big eyes, chibi
- ❌ Cyberpunk/steampunk/invented Western-fantasy elements
- ❌ Text overlaid outside the picture (subtitles, watermarks, title cards, narration overlays, opening titles and other UI-layer text — the frame must be purely visual)

> 💡 **Exception**: prop text inside the story world (a character writing, handwriting on a scroll, plaques, name boards, letters, prescriptions and other text naturally present in the scene) is **not covered by the prohibition**. When the storyboard shot description contains such content, describe its presence faithfully and require the text to be clear.
