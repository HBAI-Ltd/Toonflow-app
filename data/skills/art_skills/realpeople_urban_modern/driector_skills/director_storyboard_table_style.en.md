# Urban Storyboard-Table Constraints · Live-Action Urban Realism · Technique Reference

---

## 1. What the storyboard table is for

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into shot language. What follows covers only the constraints that live-action urban realism imposes at the storyboard-table level.

---

## 2. Light and atmosphere

- **One lighting scheme per scene** — more than two lighting schemes must not appear within a single scene, unless there is a clear narrative turn (e.g. the desk lamp goes off → cold moonlight)
- **Bind the emotion palette** — every scene binds at least 1 emotional scene (e.g. meeting with a heart-flutter/argument at work/sweet date), and keeps the main-color + secondary-color continuity within the shot group
- **Light angle specification** — for shots facing the character straight on, the light source is at 30° off-axis by default (softer); for shots from the side, adjust as the frame requires. When describing lighting you must distinguish the angle difference between straight-on and side-on
- **Match warm-cool tone to the narrative stage** — work/solitude/conflict passages stay neutral/cool; the warm tone (warm orange + cool blue background contrast) is used after the emotion has warmed up
- **Lighting transitions are an advanced device** — a gradient from natural light (A) to indoor warm light (B) = the passage of time from day to night. Mark the lighting change points in the storyboard table

---

## 3. Environmental motion

- **Environmental motion gives the frame room to breathe** — car lights flowing past the window, a computer screen flickering, steam off a coffee cup, a phone screen lighting up. Schedule at least one shot with environmental motion every 3-4 shots, so the frame does not go "dead"
- **Urban environmental elements come first** — environmental motion should use elements from inside the urban world: car lights flowing, raindrops streaking the window, coffee steam, a phone screen lighting up and going dark, a curtain stirring, lift digits ticking over; elements incompatible with the modern city are forbidden

---

## 4. Urban action rhythm

- **Urban action must be natural** — all character action defaults to a natural rhythm. Standing up, turning around, raising a hand should all be marked "natural" or "casual"
- **Prop interaction** — in urban scenes, the use of props such as phones, coffee cups, documents and car keys is a natural dynamic asset; prop-interaction detail can be added to the frame description to increase the texture of everyday life
- **Modern body language** — urban characters should carry themselves with a modern urban air: a confident gait, a casual lean, small unconscious gestures (pushing back hair, checking a watch, biting a lip)

---

## 5. Camera-movement taboos

- **Fast camera moves are banned** — whip pan (甩镜), fast push in, handheld shake clash with the character of live-action urban realism (unless it is an action/chase scene)
- **Fancy transitions are banned** — wipes, spins, venetian-blind effects and the like are incompatible with this style
