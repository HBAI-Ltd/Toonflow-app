# Storyboard-Table Japanese Anime Constraints · 1990s Japanese Anime · Technique Reference

---

## 1. Where the storyboard table sits

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into camera language. What follows provides only the constraint specification of the 1990s Japanese anime style at storyboard-table level.

---

## 2. Light and atmosphere

- **One lighting scheme per scene** — no more than two lighting schemes should appear within a single scene, unless there is an explicit narrative turn (such as the lamp going off → the cool glow of moonlight)
- **Emotion-palette binding** — every scene binds at least 1 emotional scene (such as heart-flutter meeting/everyday warmth/parting sorrow) and keeps the main + secondary colors continuous within the shot group
- **Light-source angle specification** — 30-45° oblique side light by default, simulating natural light. When describing light, the angular difference between front and side lighting must be distinguished
- **Match warm/cool tone to the narrative stage** — the first half (everyday/work/study) keeps warm tones; cool tones (cool blue + warm contrast) are used at night or after an emotional turn
- **Lighting transitions are an advanced device** — a gradual change from natural light (A) to warm interior light (B) = the passage of time from day to night. Mark the lighting change points in the storyboard table

---

## 3. Environmental motion

- **Environmental motion gives the frame room to breathe** — leaves stirring, clouds drifting, a train passing, raindrops falling, candlelight flickering. Arrange at least one shot with environmental motion every 3-4 shots, so the frame does not go "dead"
- **Japanese environmental elements first** — environmental motion should use elements from within the Japanese anime world: cherry blossoms falling, leaves stirring, clouds drifting, a train passing, raindrops falling; elements that clash with the 1990s Japanese anime style are forbidden

---

## 4. Animated action rhythm

- **Animated action must be natural** — all character action defaults to a natural rhythm. Standing up, turning, raising a hand should all be marked "natural" or "gentle"
- **Expression detail** — the micro-expressions of 1990s Japanese anime are an important means of emotional expression; small changes of expression (a smile, a flicker in the eyes, a slight lowering of the head) may be added in the shot description to make the frame feel "alive"
- **Reserved and understated** — the bearing of Japanese anime characters should be reserved and understated, with emotion expressed through detail rather than exaggerated action

---

## 5. Camera-movement taboos

- **Fast camera movement is banned** — whip pans (甩镜), sudden push-ins and handheld shake clash with the temperament of 1990s Japanese anime (unless used for a transition or an emphasis)
- **Fancy transitions are banned** — wipes, spins, venetian blinds and the like are incompatible with this style
