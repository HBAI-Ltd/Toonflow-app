# Storyboard-Table Medieval Constraints · Live-Action Medieval Epic · Technique Reference

---

## 1. Storyboard table positioning

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning script into shot language. Below are only the live-action medieval epic style constraints at the storyboard-table level.

---

## 2. Light and atmosphere

- **One scene, one lighting scheme** — a scene must not mix more than two lighting schemes unless a clear narrative turn motivates it (torches doused → moonlight)
- **Emotion palette binding** — every scene binds at least one emotional scene (judgment / pursuit / bond / vindication...) and keeps dominant + support color continuity across its shots
- **Light-source angle norms** — window and dawn shafts enter low and directional; fire sources light from below or beside at face height; moonlight is high, still, and hard-edged. Distinguish source angles when describing light
- **Warm-cold matched to narrative stage** — pursuit / judgment / exile stay cold or neutral; warmth (amber against cold stone) is released only in bond and vindication segments
- **Light transitions are an advanced device** — overcast (A) fading into hearth (C) = day into refuge. Mark light-change points in the table

---

## 3. Environment dynamics

- **Dynamics give frames breath** — banner snap in wind, torch flicker, breath fog, drifting snow, ember sparks rising, a horse shifting weight. Every 3–4 shots, at least one frame carries environment dynamics so the film never goes dead
- **Worldview-native elements only** — choose dynamics inside the medieval world: banner and cloak in wind, fire and ember movement, rain drip from eaves, crows lifting, mail rustle; never modern-world dynamics

---

## 4. Action rhythm

- **Weight over speed** — all movement defaults to deliberate and weighted: rising slowly from a bench, a sword drawn with intent, a door pushed against its age; mark actions "deliberate" or "weighted"
- **Prop interaction** — tankards, maps, whetstones, reins, cloak clasps are natural dynamic assets; add prop-interaction detail to frame descriptions for lived-in texture
- **Period body language** — bearing follows station: a soldier's square stance, a noble's stillness, a hunter's economy of motion; oaths and pledges carry full ritual weight in gesture

---

## 5. Camera taboos

- **No fast camerawork** — whip pans, crash zooms, and shaky handheld conflict with this style's solemn weight (except inside battle or pursuit peaks, used sparingly)
- **No flashy transitions** — wipes, spins, and shutter effects are incompatible; default is the hard cut, with slow fades between major acts only

---

## 6. Safe violence staging (mandatory)

> Video generation models reject graphic-harm prompts; the storyboard table must stage violence by implication.

- **Impact lands off-frame** — strikes are directed toward off-screen or behind cover; the frame holds the witness, the swing, or the aftermath — never the injury
- **The witness is the shot** — the strongest violence coverage is a face: the bond watching, the enemy realizing, the hand releasing a sword
- **Sparks, not blood** — contact reads as "blades clash, sparks on impact, cinematic slow motion"; never wounds or blood vocabulary
- **Death by stillness** — a fallen banner, an empty helm rocking, boots stopping mid-frame, crows lifting; mark such shots "aftermath, quiet"
- **Duels build by scale, not cuts** — escalate tension by changing shot scale (wide → medium → close on eyes → blades) while keeping durations full; do not shorten shots into a modern flurry

---

## 7. Creature reveal rules

- **Reveal in three steps** — part (a claw, an eye in shadow) → silhouette (shape against fog or fire) → full presence (moon rim light); never open on the full creature
- **The creature's eyes anchor emotion** — once revealed, coverage returns to the eyes; the bond with a creature is played in eye-level two-shots like any human scene
- **Practical realism guard** — describe creatures with weight and material (fur shifting, breath fog, claws on stone), never with glow or CG vocabulary
