# Medieval Style Constraints · Live-Action Medieval Epic · Technique Reference

---

## 1. Color system and image keynote

- **Tonal base** — the whole film rests on stone grey (C1), cold steel (C2), and bone snow (C6); overall temperature cold (4500–5500K), saturation low (30–50%), presenting a grim, weighty medieval world
- **Warmth is earned** — candle amber (C3) and hearth glow are the only warm sources, reserved as visual signals of bond, refuge, and vindication; cold rules pursuit, judgment, and exile. Warm-cold switching must track the emotional arc
- **Warm-cold allocation** — pursuit and judgment segments stay cold/neutral; campfire, tavern, and vindication segments release warmth; never run warm throughout or the emotional hierarchy collapses
- **Palette-first principle** — plan segments by binding the emotional scene first (betrayal / exile / bond / vindication...), then fix dominant + support colors and the lighting scheme, so the story and the color never disagree
- **Deep crimson discipline** — C4 appears only as banners, wine, and sunset; never described as blood
- **Forbidden gamut** — fluorescent and neon colors, glowing magic hues, polished gold gloss are incompatible with this style

---

## 2. Lighting scheme system

- **Light as narrative** — 6 lighting schemes map to emotional segments; the director fixes a scheme direction per segment at planning stage, not per shot
- **Sources must be motivated** — every scheme names its source: window, sky, fire, or moon

| Scheme | Name | Tonal lean | Fitting emotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Overcast diffusion | Grey base, even soft | Travel, daily life, honest labor |
| B | Hall shafts | Cold beams through high windows + dust | Judgment, councils, power |
| C | Hearth intimacy | Amber pool + black beyond | Bond, refuge, confession |
| D | Torchlit night | Moving warm pools on stone | Pursuit through halls, night watch |
| E | Moon steel | Still silver-blue, hard edges | Creature encounters, vigil, dread |
| F | Dawn vindication | Low gold shafts through smoke or dust | Release, the kneeling, endings |

- **Warm-light allocation** — warm light exists all film but layered; in betrayal and pursuit segments, cut the warm ratio so light color never contradicts emotion
- **Atmosphere mapping** — every scene's atmosphere direction must map to one scheme (A–F) for visual consistency

## 3. Texture direction

- **Documentary grit** — live-action core: visible skin pores, strand-level hair, believable material detail; wind-chapped faces, work-worn hands
- **Strong contrast + ultra-fine detail** — the image anchor; not low-contrast prettiness, not heavy-filter mood, but a filmable, landable period-drama realism
- **Material truth** — costume and props must be historically credible: wool weave, forged iron, worn leather, tallow light on stone all obey live-shoot logic
- **Skin texture** — keep pores and small flaws; restrain smoothing; pursue "real but compelling"
- **Weather presence** — breath fog, damp shoulders, mud at hems keep the world physically cold and alive
- **Restraint over spectacle** — amplify emotion through composition, scale, and rhythm, never through effects gloss

## 4. Medieval scene space elements

Scene elements specific to this worldview and their visual narrative functions:

- **Gates / archways / battlements** — natural framing devices; thresholds mark decisions and exiles
- **The guild hall's high seat and long floor** — the power axis: distance to the high seat is distance to power; the accused stands in the open center
- **Hearth / campfire** — the emotion vessel: the bond's safe circle of light inside a black world
- **Moor / forest / pass** — exposure, shelter, and peril; the wilderness is where truth lives, the hall is where power lies
- **Weather and time as transitions** — day to dusk, clear to fog, first snow can buffer segment emotion; avoid hard cuts between worlds
- **Turning points shown, not told** — prefer visual means (scale jump, eyeline break, spatial barrier) over dialogue explanation

## 5. Medieval instruments and ambient sound

Sound constraints for this worldview:

### Instrument selection

- **Low strings (cello, viol)** — the epic's base color; carries the weight of fate in daily and grim segments
- **Frame drum and war drum** — the pulse of pursuit and approaching battle; enters low, builds slow
- **War horn** — signal of armies and judgment; use sparingly, at structural moments
- **Fiddle and pipe** — tavern and village life, the only folk-warm register
- **Choral voice** — reserved for sacrifice and vindication; a single voice keening carries more than a full choir
- **Silence and wind** — the most solemn register: key deaths, decisions, and endings drop all music, keeping only wind, fire crackle, or absolute silence
- **Ambient sound has priority** — real environmental sound is the immersion key; score must never crush dialogue

### Combination strategy

| Emotional stage | Combination |
|---|---|
| Daily / travel | Low strings alone, restrained |
| Undertow / gathering | Frame drum enters under strings |
| Pursuit / battle | Drums forward + horn accents, or sudden total silence |
| Sacrifice / cost | Single voice or single viol keening |
| Vindication | Strings swell + distant choral, then quiet |
| Quiet ending | Wind and embers only |

### Ambient sound bed

- **Typical layers** — wind over heather / fire crackle / horse shifting / mail rustle / banner snap / distant wolf / tavern murmur / rain on thatch / boots on stone
- **Mark 1–2 core ambient sounds per scene** to guide later sound design; the truer the ambient layer, the stronger the period realism

## 6. Violence and content-safety direction (planning level)

- **Grading carries the blood** — the feeling of violence lives in cold steel tones, hard contrast, and aftermath imagery, never in explicit injury
- **Impact off-frame** — plan every strike to land off-screen or behind cover; the witness's face is the shot
- **Aftermath in metaphor** — torn banners, broken shields, a riderless horse, crows, grey snow; no bodies, no blood pools
- **Asset protection** — plan no scene that would require a bloodied character reference; assets stay clean so generation references never poison later shots
