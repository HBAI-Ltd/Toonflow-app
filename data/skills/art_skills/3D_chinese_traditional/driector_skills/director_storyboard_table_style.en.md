# Storyboard-Table Guofeng 3D Constraints · Guofeng 3D · Technique Reference

---

## 1. Where the storyboard table sits

The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into camera language. What follows provides only the constraint specification of the Guofeng 3D style at storyboard-table level.

---

## 2. Lighting and atmosphere

- **One lighting scheme per scene** — no more than two lighting schemes should appear within a single scene, unless there is an explicit narrative turn (such as a natural light change from day to night or from clear to rain)
- **Emotion-palette binding** — every scene binds at least 1 emotional scene (courtly opulence/landscape mood/boudoir gentleness/wuxia grimness/festive celebration/moonlit night stillness) and keeps the main + secondary colors continuous within the shot group
- **3D render lighting specification** — when describing the lighting you must state the direction and strength of the volumetric light, the layering of ambient occlusion, and the range of depth-of-field blur. How PBR materials reflect/refract under different lighting is the key to the texture of the frame
- **Match warm/cool tone to the narrative stage** — grim/melancholy/oppressive passages use cool light (indigo + ink black); warm tones such as vermilion/golden yellow are banned there. Warm tones (vermilion + golden yellow + rouge) are used only in opulent, gentle and festive passages
- **Lighting transition is an advanced device** — gradating from diffusion through window gauze (E) to the clear light of a moonlit night (F) = the passage of time from day to night. Mark the lighting change points in the storyboard table

---

## 3. Environmental dynamics

- **Environmental motion gives the frame room to breathe** — petals drifting down, smoke rising, water rippling, gauze curtains stirring, dust motes in a volumetric light shaft. Every 3-4 shots, schedule at least one shot with environmental motion so the frame does not go "dead"
- **Guofeng-era environmental elements come first** — environmental motion should use elements from within the Guofeng-era world: falling blossom, drifting catkins, light smoke, running water, wind through a bamboo grove, a flickering candle flame; modern elements are forbidden
- **3D particles and volumetric effects** — the environmental dynamics of Guofeng 3D should exploit the advantages of 3D rendering to the full: volumetric light scattering in mist, PBR reflection on a water surface, the physical falling trajectory of blossom, volumetric rendering of smoke

---

## 4. Guofeng-era action rhythm

- **Guofeng-era action must be slow** — all character action defaults to slow. Standing up, turning around and raising a hand should all be marked "slow"
- **Costume dynamics** — the fluttering of Guofeng-era costume (wide sleeves, skirt hem, silk shawl) is a natural motion asset; the drape and flutter of cloth simulation in the 3D render should be reflected in the shot description, adding a "living" feel to the frame
- **Deportment constraint** — the bearing of Guofeng-era characters should fit the temperament of the period: walking steady and unhurried, gestures reserved and restrained, no modernized body language (such as shrugging or throwing up the hands)
- **3D animation texture** — action transitions should be natural and fluid, showing the refinement of 3D animation. Avoid stiff pose switching; watch the natural arc of the joints and the shift of the center of gravity

---

## 5. Camera-movement taboos

- **Fast camera movement is banned** — whip pan (甩镜), abrupt push-in and handheld shake clash with the elegant temperament of Guofeng 3D
- **Fancy transitions are banned** — wipe, rotation, venetian blind and the like are incompatible with this style
- **Use 3D spatial camera movement well** — you may use the spatial depth of the 3D scene for a slow push-pull/orbit (环绕) camera movement, but it must keep a steady, graceful rhythm; show-off fly-through/flying camera movement is forbidden
