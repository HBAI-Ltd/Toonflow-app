# Storyboard-Table Guofeng Cyber 3D Constraints · Guofeng Cyber 3D · Technique Reference
---

## 1. Where the storyboard table sits
The storyboard table is the director's core tool for turning the script into camera language. What follows provides only the constraint specification of the Guofeng Cyber 3D style at storyboard-table level. **The core principle is "Guofeng core first, cyber form second": whether the scene is classical ancient-style or modern urban, you must hold to the elegant bearing of classical Eastern aesthetics and to narrative logic, and cyber elements must never steal the show.**

---

## 2. Core principles common to both scene types (mandatory for ancient-style/urban alike)
1.  **Iron law of style proportion**: in every shot, Guofeng core elements (traditional colors, Eastern forms, classical bearing, Chinese aesthetics) make up ≥70% and cyber tech elements ≤30%; purely Western cyber and coreless tech pile-ups are ruled out
2.  **Adaptation logic for the two scene types**:
    - Classical ancient-style scenes: build on traditional Chinese architecture/costume/etiquette, and use cyber elements as narrative accents that do not break the integrity of the ancient-style world
    - Modern urban scenes: build on neo-Chinese aesthetics and a traditional Eastern core, fuse cyber elements into the urban setting, and you must plant concrete Guofeng signifiers (neo-Chinese forms, traditional colors, Eastern etiquette, Guofeng motifs); pure urban cyber with no Guofeng core is forbidden
3.  **Unified rhythm rule**: in ancient-style and urban scenes alike, the whole piece keeps by default the unhurried, elegant Eastern rhythm; only passages of strong narrative conflict may break it temporarily, and they must be annotated with an explicit duration and trigger logic

---

## 3. Lighting and atmosphere (fully applicable to both ancient-style and urban scenes)
- **One lighting scheme per scene** — no more than two lighting schemes should appear within a single scene, unless there is an explicit narrative turn (such as a natural light change from day to night or from clear to rain, or a switch between reality and a digital illusion); cyber-specific lighting (neon, holographic projection, self-emitting electronic screens, glowing components of cybernetic prosthetics) must be brought into that single unified scheme for the scene. In **ancient-style scenes**, the dominant neon color and the color temperature of electronic light within one scene must stay consistent with the ancient-style light sources (lanterns, candle flame, daylight); in **urban scenes**, the cyber light sources within one scene must match the color temperature of the Chinese-style base light sources (neo-Chinese pendant lamps, Guofeng street lamps, Chinese-style rim light on buildings). Stray mixed-color light with no narrative logic is forbidden
- **Emotion-palette binding** — every scene binds at least 1 emotional scene, covering both scene types:
  - Ancient-style base set: courtly opulence/landscape mood/boudoir gentleness/wuxia grimness/festive celebration/moonlit night stillness
  - Guofeng Cyber exclusive set (shared by ancient-style and urban): cyber-jianghu grimness/digital-illusion hallucination/cyber-court power struggle/wasteland ancient-architecture desolation/neon night-market bustle/Guofeng urban workplace/urban old-alley intrigue
  and keeps the main + secondary colors continuous within the shot group. The palette rule is mandatory: "traditional Guofeng colors as the base". Core adaptation color pairings for reference: vermilion with neon cyan, malachite blue with fluorescent pink, ink black with electronic purple, gilt gold with ice blue. Losing the Guofeng core is ruled out
- **3D render lighting specification** — when describing the lighting you must state the direction and strength of the volumetric light, the layering of ambient occlusion, and the range of depth-of-field blur. How PBR materials reflect/refract under different lighting is the key to the texture of the frame. Render specifications specific to each scene type:
  - Ancient-style scenes: you must state the PBR material parameters of the wooden structure/brick and stone/silk/jade of the ancient architecture, and at the same time annotate the semi-transparent volumetric-light tiers of holographic projection, the color-bleed control of neon tubing, and the reflection of metal components
  - Urban scenes: you must state the PBR material parameters of the glass curtain wall/concrete/metal panels of neo-Chinese architecture, and at the same time annotate the light reflection of Guofeng motifs etched into the glass, the volumetric-light scattering of Guofeng holographic advertising, the strength and range of the Chinese-style rim light on buildings, and the glow radius of the light-emitting components of cybernetic prosthetics/wearable devices
  Every lighting description must state the logic by which Guofeng materials and cyber materials fuse in light, so the two do not split apart
- **Match warm/cool tone to the narrative stage** — grim/melancholy/oppressive/intrigue passages use cool light (indigo + ink black + low-saturation neon cyan); highly saturated warm tones such as vermilion/golden yellow are banned there. Warm tones (vermilion + golden yellow + rouge + low-saturation neon pink/orange) are used only in opulent, gentle and festive passages. Electronic glitch light and strobe may be used only at points of strong narrative conflict (such as a system crash or a memory flashback), and their duration and frequency must be annotated; **overuse is forbidden in ancient-style and urban scenes alike**
- **Lighting transition is an advanced device** — transition specification shared by both scene types; you must mark the lighting change points:
  - Ancient-style scenes: diffusion through window gauze gradating to the clear light of a moonlit night = the passage of time from day to night; the warm light of a lantern gradating to the cool light of holographic projection = the switch from reality to a digital illusion
  - Urban scenes: the warm light of Chinese-style street lamps gradating to the cool neon of an office tower = the scene switch from daily life to the workplace; the light through an old alley's window lattice gradating to the light on a skyscraper's glass curtain wall = the narrative transition from tradition to modernity
  Every lighting transition must keep an unhurried rhythm, matching the elegant Guofeng temperament

## 4. Environmental dynamics (fully applicable to both ancient-style and urban scenes)
- **Environmental motion gives the frame room to breathe** — every 3-4 shots, schedule at least one shot with environmental motion so the frame does not go "dead". **Mandatory for both scene types**: every 3-4 shots you must express the fusion of "Guofeng core motion elements" + "cyber tech motion elements" together; splitting a shot into elements of one scene type only is forbidden:
  - Ancient-style adaptation: falling petals accompanied by drifting holographic particles, a flickering candle flame accompanied by the faint flicker of an electronic screen, a stirring gauze curtain accompanied by the flow of holographic projection light, wind through a bamboo grove accompanied by the breathing glow of neon tubing among the bamboo
  - Urban adaptation: the canvas of a neo-Chinese awning stirring in the wind accompanied by neon light flowing under the eaves, holographic Guofeng calligraphy particles drifting through a shopping district accompanied by leaves falling through the air, the shifting light of Guofeng motifs on an office tower's glass curtain wall accompanied by the slight movement of the leaves of an indoor Chinese-style potted plant, the breathing glow of a Guofeng light box in a subway passage accompanied by the wind of a passing train
- **Guofeng environmental elements come first** — the core of the environmental motion must use elements from within the Guofeng world, covering both scene types:
  - Compliant elements for ancient-style scenes: falling blossom, drifting catkins, light smoke, running water, wind through a bamboo grove, a flickering candle flame, a stirring gauze curtain, rippling water
  - Compliant elements for urban scenes: neo-Chinese components moving in the wind, holographic motion of Guofeng motifs, the swaying leaves of a Chinese-style potted plant, the swaying light of Guofeng lanterns/street lamps, running water in a Chinese-style water feature, fluttering rice paper/silk
  Cyber motion elements are limited to content compliant with the Guofeng Cyber world: holographic motion of Guofeng motifs, the breathing glow of neon tubing, digital particles flowing into calligraphy/landscape forms, the slow motion of mechanical components, the faint flicker of cybernetic-prosthetic parts, the slight hovering sway of floating Guofeng architecture. **Taboos shared by both scene types**: street graffiti, purely Western billboards, modern cars with no Guofeng core, Western cyber machinery and other jarring elements are forbidden
- **3D particles and volumetric effects** — exploit the advantages of 3D rendering to the full to fuse Guofeng and cyber elements in volumetric effects; adaptation specification for the two scene types:
  - Ancient-style scenes: volumetric light scattering in mist, PBR reflection of ancient architecture on a water surface, the physical falling trajectory of blossom, volumetric rendering of smoke, with holographic Guofeng calligraphy particles and holographic petal trail light nested in
  - Urban scenes: volumetric light scattering in rain mist, PBR reflection of Guofeng motifs on a glass curtain wall, the physical falling trajectory of leaves, volumetric rendering of holographic projection, with holographic Guofeng landscape particles and Guofeng-contour scattering of neon light nested in
  Every particle effect must be annotated with its count, volumetric density and movement speed, matching the elegant slow Guofeng rhythm. **In both scene types, irregular particle effects flying about at high speed are forbidden**

## 5. Ancient-style action rhythm (fully applicable to both ancient-style and urban scenes)
- **Core ancient-style action rhythm: slow, steady, restrained** — all character action defaults to slow. Standing up, turning around and raising a hand should all be marked "slow". Cyber-specific actions (deploying a cybernetic prosthetic, holographic operation, extension and retraction of mechanical components) likewise default to slow and graceful. **Only in passages of strong narrative conflict such as a fight or a crisis may fast action be marked, and the duration of that fast action must not exceed 30% of the shot's duration.** **Additional specification for urban scenes**: even in daily commuting, workplace and urban social scenes, character action must keep the steady, unhurried Eastern bearing; hurried short steps and large fast movements are banned, the restrained Guofeng bearing is kept throughout, and flashy mechanical show-off moves are banned
- **Costume dynamics** — the fluttering of Guofeng costume is a natural motion asset; the drape and flutter of cloth simulation in the 3D render should be reflected in the shot description, adding a "living" feel to the frame. Adaptation specification for the two scene types:
  - Ancient-style scenes: you must annotate the drape and flutter of the silk/cotton-linen/gauze cloth of traditional ancient-style costume (wide sleeves, skirt hem, silk shawl), and at the same time annotate the linked motion of neon tubing, glowing motifs and mechanical accessories on the costume
  - Urban scenes: you must annotate the drape and flutter of the cloth of neo-Chinese costume (modernized qipao, neo-Chinese suit, Guofeng everyday wear), and at the same time annotate the linked motion of glowing frog buttons, mechanical cuffs, holographic motifs and Guofeng accessories on the costume
  Taboo shared by both scene types: cyber costume components must not break the core form of Guofeng costume or its Eastern beauty
- **Deportment constraint** — deportment specification shared by both scene types: a character's bearing must fit classical Eastern temperament — walking steady and unhurried, gestures reserved and restrained, no modernized body language (such as shrugging, throwing up the hands, jiggling a leg, waving broadly, or exaggerated swinging of the limbs). **Additional specification for urban scenes**: cyber-related body actions (operating a holographic panel, badging through access control, adjusting a cybernetic prosthetic, operating electronic devices) must stay reserved and graceful, with slow, steady, precise operating movements; exaggerated broad gestures and Western body language must not appear. Even for a character with mechanical prosthetics, body action must fit the classical Guofeng bearing and avoid a cold, stiff mechanical feel
- **3D animation texture** — action transitions should be natural and fluid, showing the refinement of 3D animation. Avoid stiff pose switching; watch the natural arc of the joints and the shift of the center of gravity. Additional specification for the two scene types: the transition between traditional body action and cyber mechanical action must be naturally smooth, with no stutter or dropped frames; mechanical-prosthetic action must match the human center of gravity and joint motion logic; holographic operation must have explicit spatial positioning and feedback animation, holding together the realistic texture of 3D animation and the evocative Eastern bearing of Guofeng

## 6. Camera-movement and transition taboos (fully applicable to both ancient-style and urban scenes)
- **Fast camera movement is banned** — whip pan (甩镜), abrupt push-in, handheld shake, high-speed fly-through, strobing jump cuts and first-person fast-running camera movement all clash with the elegant temperament of Guofeng 3D; **banned by default throughout, in ancient-style and urban scenes alike**. Only within a single shot of strong narrative conflict (an explosion, a system crash, a life-or-death fight) may extremely short fast camera movement be used, and its duration must not exceed 20% of that shot's total duration; breaking the elegant Eastern temperament to show off is forbidden
- **Fancy transitions are banned** — wipe, rotation, venetian blind, hard cut to white, glitch-art garbled transitions with no narrative logic and zoom-bounce transitions are all incompatible with this style; **banned throughout in ancient-style and urban scenes alike**. Only transitions that fit the Guofeng Cyber temperament may be used: lighting-gradation transition, element-match transition (an ancient flying eave matched to a cyber flying eave, a jade pendant matched to a Guofeng access card, a lantern matched to a Guofeng street lamp), spatial-depth push-pull transition, slow dissolve transition. The transition rhythm must be unhurried, matching the overall Guofeng temperament
- **Use 3D spatial camera movement well (adaptation specification for the two scene types)** — you may use the spatial depth of the 3D scene for a slow push-pull/orbit (环绕) camera movement, but it must keep a steady, graceful rhythm; show-off fly-through/flying camera movement is forbidden. Camera-movement guidance specific to each scene type:
  - Ancient-style scenes: you may run a slow orbit (环绕) camera movement around an ancient architectural courtyard, presenting the fusion of ancient-style architecture with cyber elements; push in slowly from the courtyard gatehead so the cyberized ancient-style interior emerges gradually in the depth of the frame, camera speed ≤0.5m/s by default
  - Urban scenes: you may push in slowly from the Chinese-style gatehead of an urban old alley so that skyscrapers and a neon shopping district planted with Guofeng elements emerge gradually in the depth of the frame; you may run a slow orbit (环绕) camera movement around a neo-Chinese office tower, presenting the building's Chinese-style flying eaves together with the cyber light on its glass curtain wall. Camera movement must serve narrative and emotional expression throughout rather than being pure effect display; push-pull/orbit (环绕) speed ≤0.5m/s by default
