# Flat Style Constraints · 2D Flat Design · Technique Reference

---

## 1. Tonal system and visual keynote

- **Tonal base** — the whole film uses bright blue (C1), vivid orange (C2) and off-white (C9) as its base colors, with a neutral overall color temperature (5500-6500K) and medium-high saturation (70-90%), giving the fresh, clean feel of modern flat style
- **Warm/cool narrative contrast** — warm colors (vivid orange C2, warm pink C7) are the visual signal of rising feeling, used to reinforce heart-flutter and romantic passages locally. Cool colors (bright blue C1, pale purple C6) serve everyday and night passages. Warm/cool switches should track the story arc, not be mixed at random
- **Warm/cool allocation strategy** — everyday/office passages keep a neutral-to-cool tonal base; warm color is released only after the feeling rises, using warm/cool contrast as the narrative turn
- **Palette-first principle** — passage planning must bind an emotional scene first (everyday/heart-flutter/romantic/memory, etc.), then settle the main + secondary colors and the color-block scheme, to avoid "the plot is right but the emotion is in the wrong color"
- **Banned color range** — highly saturated fluorescent colors, neon colors and 3D-render color systems are all incompatible with this style

---

## 2. Color-block scheme system

- **Color blocks are narrative** — the 6 color-block schemes correspond to different emotional passages; in the planning stage the director should set the color-block direction at passage level rather than shot by shot
- **Flat style has no traditional light-and-shadow** — "pseudo light-and-shadow" is created through lightness differences between color blocks, with no light-source angle

| Color-block scheme | Scheme name | Tonal tendency | Applicable emotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Solid color blocks | Warm orange + off-white + light blue | Everyday warmth |
| B | Color-block contrast | Warm pink + warm orange contrast | Heart-flutter moment, rising passage |
| C | Cool tone | Cool blue + gray + white | Office/study, calm passage |
| D | Warm-dominant | Warm orange + warm pink + light yellow | Romantic scene, sweet passage |
| E | Cool-dominant | Deep blue + purple + warm yellow accents | Quiet night |
| F | Low saturation | Light yellow + light gray + warm pink | Memory/flashback |

- **Warm/cool allocation** — warm color blocks (vivid orange/warm pink) suit passages where feeling rises; calm/night passages use cool color blocks. The director may adjust the warm/cool switch point as the narrative requires
- **Atmosphere direction mapping** — the atmosphere direction of every scene should map to one of the color-block schemes above (A-F), keeping the visuals consistent

---

## 3. Texture direction

- **Geometric minimalism** — the core of flat design: solid color blocks, clear outlines, no shadow and no gradient, well-defined color-block boundaries
- **Clean lines + pure color** — this is the anchor of the visual texture. Not photorealistic rendering, not complex detail, but geometricised minimal expression
- **No realistic elements** — every visual element must fit the flat style: no shadow, no gradient, no texture, no light-and-shadow. "3D feel" and "realistic feel" are forbidden
- **Clear color-block contrast** — boundaries between color blocks are distinct, the contrast between main and secondary color is clear, blurred transitions are avoided
- **Flat does not mean monotonous** — emotion is carried through color contrast and compositional design rather than realistic detail. Use color-block size and hue change to create visual layering

---

## 4. Flat scene space elements

Scene elements specific to the flat style and their visual narrative function:

- **Solid-color background/color-block zoning** — a natural carrier of composition; use color-block areas to divide spatial layers, creating a clean sense of space and pictorial depth
- **Geometric shapes/icon symbols** — the semantic tool of flat style: circle = warmth/wholeness, triangle = dynamism/conflict, square = stability/everyday
- **Negative space** — an important emotional device of flat style. A large empty area = focus, a fully filled color block = fullness, local negative space = room to breathe
- **Solid-color transitions between passages** — this style has rich color-block assets (variants in hue/lightness); for passage joins, a solid color block is recommended as an emotional buffer rather than a hard cut
- **Turning points use color-block change** — prefer pictorial means (lightness change, hue change, an abrupt shift in color-block contrast) over explaining through dialogue

## 5. Flat-style instruments and ambient sound

Constraints on sound elements under the flat style:

### Instrument choice

- **Electronic synthesiser** — the core instrument for everyday/cosy/modern passages, best at conveying the cleanness of flat style
- **Piano** — for romantic/emotional passages; its clean timbre suits a minimalist atmosphere
- **Light, brisk rhythm** — for bright/energetic passages, suited to fast-paced scenes
- **Strings** — a bed under passages where emotion rises; should not overshadow the lead

### Instrument combination strategy

| Emotional stage | Instrument combination |
|---|---|
| Steady/opening/closing | Electronic sound solo |
| Everyday warmth | Synthesiser + light piano |
| Emotional lift/romance | Piano + strings |
| Key emotional moment | Ambient sound only (score removed) |

### Flat-style ambient sound

- **Typical ambient sound layers** — city traffic / office keyboards / café background / wind in the curtains / low murmur of a crowd / mouse clicks
- **Mark 1-2 core ambient sounds for every scene**, to help the later sound design. The richer the ambient sound layers, the more immersive the scene
